## 1. The scope

**The question is answerable but posed one level too high.** "Anything like the model's history switch" bundles three claims whose biological counterparts are different:

- **A. Multistability.** Does the head-steering and command circuit have more than one stable or persistent state, and by what mechanism: intrinsic conductance, circuit, or neuromodulator?
- **B. History selects the state.** Does sensory history choose which state the circuit ends in, and where is the history held: sensory neuron, interneuron, or modulator?
- **C. The structural claim.** Is a dense head core, with RIA as the fragile node, matched by anything known about hub neurons and brain-wide dynamics?

Questions 1 to 3 cover A and B. C is missing, and C is the thread's actual finding (M4d, M4e: the block's density and weight concentration raise the switch rate). The biology on C exists and is recent: hub neurons and non-local graph features predict brain-wide correlations and hub inhibition disrupts them [16]; the rich club of the measured signalling network differs from the anatomical rich club [15b]; the exact wiring differs substantially between isogenic individuals [17]. Add a fourth question for C.

**Reverse the exclusion of whole-brain states.** The best-documented bistability in the worm's head is the forward/reverse state held by AVA, AIB and RIM [7, 8, 10], and RIM, AIB, RIB and RIS are in C3. The global cycle of Kato et al. includes the turn phase to which the read-out neurons belong [8]. Keep global states wherever a C3 neuron carries them, which is most of the time.

**Add a model-side precedent section, half a page.** The scope's closing sentence says the results otherwise "stay facts about this model". That is not known. Kunert-Graf et al. reported multistability and long transients in a rate model on the connectome with identical neurons, including bistability between a limit cycle and a fixed point under compound input [14]. Izquierdo and Beer evolved continuous-time rate networks on a connectome-derived head-steering circuit, the nearest precedent to 02's champions [14b]. Without these the report cannot say whether the switch is new even among rate models on this wiring.

**Make the sensory layer explicit in question 2.** The probe's history enters at AWA, AWC and ASE. In the worm these differ in sign and dynamics: AWA responds to odour increases and desensitises [12c], AWC-ON responds to odour removal through an adaptive threshold [12a], ASER responds to salt decreases and encodes the cultivation set point [12d]. Much of the worm's "history" is already computed there. Question 2 should ask where the history is held, not only whether steering depends on it.

**Add a "not found" level** beside full text, abstract, snippet and wrong. A bounded search that finds nothing is a different outcome from a source that contradicts.

**Which model features are worth comparing.** Worth it: the existence and mechanism of multistability among C3 neurons; RIA's role as integrator versus store; the dorsal/ventral opposition of the read-out, since the worm has mutual inhibition between motor modules [10b] and SAA interneurons cross-connecting dorsal and ventral head motor neurons [18b]; and the hub/density claim. Not worth it: the tick time scale; the time constants; the switch rate over random genomes, which has no counterpart because the worm is one parameter set; the left/right mapping of dorsal/ventral; the symmetric gap junctions; the sensory neurons' responses; the particular ramp.

## 3. Expected answers and pitfalls

**Question 1, bistability.** Expect a yes with the wrong mechanism. RMD has documented plateau potentials and bistable membrane states under current injection [4], attributed in models to intrinsic T-type calcium current [5]; that is intrinsic conductance, which the model excludes by construction. RIM holds depolarised and hyperpolarised states reinforced by gap junctions and neuromodulators [6]. The command circuit is a flip-flop of mutual inhibition [10, 10b]. Nothing I know reports RIA holding a state; its compartmental activity tracks head position through motor feedback [1, 2, 3]. One 2026 abstract says SMD "sustains bending states" [18d], on the head-bend timescale. Confidence: high that RMD and RIM are reported as bistable or persistent, high that RIA is not, moderate on SMD.

**Question 2, history in steering.** Expect history dependence on the seconds-to-tens-of-seconds scale, held mostly in the sensory neurons [12a, 12b, 12c], in the network state that gates the sensory response [7], and in motor-to-sensory feedback [13]; and on the hours scale as plasticity [12d], which the scope excludes. A food-level history latched for tens of seconds inside the SMD, RMD, RIA circuit: expect "not found". One preprint reports SMDD and SMDV clusters responding tens of seconds after bacterial stimulus changes, which is the nearest thing and is unreviewed. Confidence: moderate.

**Question 3, RIA.** Expect a consistent picture: a sensorimotor integrator whose two axonal compartments encode head bend through acetylcholine from SMD, whose sensory input arrives globally through glutamate and nicotinic receptors, whose sensory encoding is gated by head orientation, and whose output is routed dorsally or ventrally [1, 2, 3]. The only "memory" is experience-dependent modulation of that integration [3], which is learning. Confidence: high.

**Question 4 and the pitfalls.** What would mislead:

- **Mechanism mismatch.** "Bistable" in RMD means an intrinsic plateau [4, 5]; in the model it means recurrent saturating synapses. Matching the word proves nothing.
- **RIA confirmation bias.** RIA's prominence in the literature will look like support for M4c. The known RIA functions are moment-to-moment integration gated by posture, not storage. The report should state this guard in advance.
- **Signs.** The model draws random signs per edge and runs without Dale's law by default, confirmed in `config.py`. RIA is glutamatergic, SMD and RMD cholinergic, RME GABAergic, RIM tyraminergic and glutamatergic, and glutamate is inhibitory at some worm receptors. The comparison is topological only.
- **Anatomy is not function.** Measured signal propagation departs from anatomy, with fast peptidergic signalling where no synapse exists [15a], and the signalling rich club is not the anatomical one [15b]. A connectome-constrained model can still fit the atlas well [15c]. The head core is an anatomical object; the report must not assume it is a functional one.
- **Neuromodulators hold the worm's states.** Tyramine suppresses the head oscillation and coordinates turn sequences [11]; neuropeptides switch SMD's motor states [11]; persistent behavioural states are a neuromodulatory story in general. The model has none of this.
- **The sensory layer computes history.** The ramp's two histories would evoke opposite-signed responses in AWA and ASEL against AWC and ASER [12a to 12d]. The model injects the same signal into all six as leaky integrators.
- **Embodiment and the head oscillation.** SMDD senses stretch [18a]; RIA's compartments are driven by motor feedback [1]; steering is a phase-dependent sampling across the head sweep [2, 3]. The probe runs open loop with no oscillation, so the worm's steering computation has no counterpart in it.
- **Time.** Ticks are uncalibrated and calcium indicators are slow. The natural unit in the worm is the head-bend period, which the model lacks. Do not compare "a few tens of ticks" with any number of seconds.
- **Population versus individual.** "Fewer than 2% of random genomes" is a statement about a weight ensemble. The worm is one parameter set with inter-individual wiring variation [17] of an order that could swamp the sub-percent wiring effects M4d and M5 chase.
- **Lumping RMD with SMD.** The read-out treats them as one; the worm separates fast RMD head casts from SMD's slower sustained bending [18d] and gives SMD proprioception [18a].
