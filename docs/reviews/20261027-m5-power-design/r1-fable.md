**Verdict: revise.** The skeleton is right: one primary contrast, direction as the primary field, the reading on resolved genomes, a simulation of the whole procedure, pre-registration. Three things would make the host count wrong as proposed: the variance input rests on one of three available sources and the sources disagree by a factor of about ten in the host component; the decision rule minimizes hosts rather than compute; and two parts of the "exact reading procedure" to be simulated, the guard and the envelope, do not work at 60 hosts. Ranked points follow, then the answers by question.

## Ranked points

**1. The host variance is the biggest lever, and it is not known to a factor of ten.** I recomputed the per-host spreads and their binomial genome-sampling part from the committed per-host counts, using the pooled rates and 1024 genomes per host.

| Source | Contrast | Per-host SD | Genome-sampling part | Host plus draw residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M4e `readings.json:263` | T − K | 0.0055 | 41% | 59% |
| M4d `readings.json:28` | T − R | 0.0035 | 88% | 12% |
| M4d `readings.json:64` | T − Rr | 0.0033 | 83% | 17% |

Astra's 51/8/41 is the M4e row only. M4d's two contrasts, including the one closest to K\*, show per-host spread barely above pure genome sampling. At 10 hosts neither estimate is trustworthy, and one host drives each (SH-2011100 in M4e, SH-recip-2051000 in M4d). The required H for 0.002 at 80% power, one-sided 2.5%, D = 1, G = 1024, by the normal approximation:

| Host residual assumed | K\* rate like K's 0.0054 | K\* rate like Rr's 0.0031 |
|---|---|---|
| M4d-like | about 28 | about 23 |
| M4e-like | about 59 | about 54 |

So the answer to the owner's question is somewhere between about 25 and about 60 hosts, and the proposal's inputs cannot say where. Fix: fit the variance to all three sources, report H as a range, and run a K\* pilot on M4e's ten existing hosts before the final calculation. The T panels already exist, so only the K\* panels are new: 20 panels at about 0.25 h each, about 5 to 10 CPU hours. It gives T − K\* on 20 host-draws, K\*'s base rate, its unresolved rate, its S3 distribution and its edge overlap with T, all of which the calculation needs and none of which T − K can supply.

**2. The K\* base-rate anchor is wrong by half.** The proposal anchors K\* at K's 55 of 10,240. But K\* is M4d's Rr rewiring with autapses, and Rr had 32 of 10,240 (`M4D-RESULTS.md:122`). If K\*'s rate is Rr-like, T − K\* is near 0.003 rather than 0.002, and H at 0.003 drops to roughly 10 to 25. Use both anchors until the pilot decides. Note also that K\* can be built from existing pieces: Rr's swap rule (`M4D-PLAN.md:63`, 51,603 attempts for 5200 accepted swaps, so feasibility is settled), plus N2's autapses as every M4d block arm had, plus either K's weight shuffle or Rr's out-strengths kept. I would keep out-strengths, the tighter null, and state the choice. Report the edge overlap between each K\* draw and T: a degree-preserving null on a block of density 0.3 shares a large fraction of T's edges by construction, more so with reciprocity fixed, and that dilutes "exact wiring" in a way the reader should see.

**3. The decision rule should minimize compute, not hosts.** Under any of the decompositions above, draw variance is small, so D = 2 at a given H buys little, and at fixed host-draws D = 1 beats D = 2 by about 1.5 in variance. Likewise, at fixed total genome-panels, more hosts with fewer genomes each is never worse, because the host component does not shrink with G. The grid should include D = 1 and G = 512 as serious candidates, and the headline should be "the cheapest allocation reaching 80% at 0.002, and its H", with the H at M4e's design (D = 2, G = 1024) beside it for comparison. Cost: M4e's K and T panels ran about 0.25 h each including the second pass, so a two-arm M5 is about 0.5 h per host-draw, not 1.05. At D = 1 that is about 30 h for 60 hosts and 50 h for 100.

**4. The guard and the envelope do not scale to M5's H.** The simulation is supposed to apply the exact reading procedure, so both must be redefined first.
- The guard (`m4e_read.py:85-86`, `M4E-PLAN.md:192`) voids an exclusion if any host's difference reaches 0.01. Under the null with a per-host SD of 0.0055, that is about 7% per host, so at 60 hosts it fires in about 98% of null runs. It only touches the exclusion sub-field, not direction, but it would make every exclusion in M5 read "a single host responds". Replace it with something that scales: a leave-one-host-out check, or a rule on the share of hosts above zero.
- The envelope (`m4e_read.py:57-65`) enumerates all 2^H corners for the Student-t interval. At H = 60 that is 10^18 corners. Define a computable conservative version: bootstrap at the two ends, and for the t-interval a bound using the all-low mean with the largest achievable spread.

**5. The target of 0.002 is right, but anchor it differently.** The proposal says neither observed value is the target, yet 0.002 is visibly where they sit. Two better anchors exist. First, 0.002 is about a third of T − I and the size of each of M4e's three steps, so the question becomes "does the exact wiring carry as much as the weight shape or the degrees do". Second, 0.002 is the smallest effect this classifier can honestly resolve: the audit bounds each arm's missed-transition rate at about 0.002 with 1500 audited genomes, and the unresolved share per arm is about 0.003, so a 0.001 claim would be decided by classification limits, not by hosts. That is also the answer to whether 0.001 should be the target: no, not with this classifier, at any H. Replace the 0.004 exclusion with an exclusion at 0.002 as the secondary size field, so that a null result says "no effect of 0.002 or more"; at effect zero and the planned precision, that exclusion has roughly the same 80% rate as detection has at 0.002. Report 90% power too; it costs about 1.34 times the hosts.

**6. The unresolved genomes: agree with the resolved-genome primary, with three additions.**
- Add "not fading" (separate states plus persistent oscillation plus unresolved) as a secondary outcome. It has no unresolved problem at all, and M4e's T has 30 persistent oscillations against K's 10, a difference as large as the separate-states effect. If the exact wiring's effect shows there and not in separate states among resolved genomes, that is a different finding, and the pre-registration should say how it will be read.
- On bias: the resolved reading is unbiased if the unresolved genomes are a real slow class at this horizon rather than hidden switches. The dangerous case is K\* having more unresolved genomes than T, since hidden switches there inflate T − K\*. Pre-register a qualifier: the reading stands as "among genomes resolved at the horizon", and if K\*'s unresolved share exceeds T's by more than some fixed amount, the reading is qualified. The envelope stays a sensitivity.
- On the third pass versus hosts: they buy different things, hosts shrink the interval and the pass shrinks the envelope. At H = 60 a 100,000-tick pass on about 400 unresolved genomes would cost about 40 h, as much as the run itself. Pilot it first on M4e's 70 unresolved T and K genomes, about 7 h: if most settle as fading, a longer second pass in M5 nearly removes the problem; if not, they are a real class and the conditional estimand is the honest one. That pilot is exploratory and does not touch M4e's published reading.

**7. The genome pairing gives no variance reduction, and the model should say so.** M4d found the same genome switching in two arms once in 60 or more (`M4D-RESULTS.md:185`). So the paired panels do not reduce the contrast's variance; the per-host genome component is the sum of two independent binomial variances. The simulation should model it that way, and the pilot should confirm it on M4e's T and K panels by counting shared switch ids.

**8. Fit the variance on the differences, not a GLMM.** With two draws and sparse counts, a logit GLMM with arm-by-host and arm-by-draw terms is overspecified. A one-way random-effects fit on the per-host-draw differences gives the same three components: between-draw variance within hosts estimates draw plus genome, between-host variance of the host means estimates host plus the rest over D, and the genome part is analytic from the rates. Propagate the host component as a range from the sources in point 1, not only by host bootstrap, which cannot see beyond ten hosts. Per-host base rates for the "proportional" scenario should come from a fitted overdispersed distribution: T's per-host counts run from 2 to 17 of 1024, far beyond binomial spread.

**9. Audit sizing bounds the differential at the effect's size.** With zero transitions in 1500 per arm, each arm's rate is bounded at 0.002, so the bound on the difference between arms is also 0.002, equal to the target. Either state that as a limit beside the reading, or go to about 3000 per arm, which is cheap: M4e's audited fading genomes cost a few seconds each, so 6000 is a few hours.

**10. Pre-registration: yes, with one review round on the power output.** The power script's inputs decide everything, so reviewers should re-derive its fitted components and its calibration check. The calibration check is also the natural sabotage check: simulate M4e's design at the fitted parameters and confirm the simulated T − K half-width covers the observed 0.0039 and the unresolved rates. Then M5's plan gets its own rounds. Two further pre-registration items: a 30-to-60-hour CPU run exceeds every previous cap, so register the batches and the rule that nothing is read before the last batch finishes, or a group-sequential rule with its alpha spending; and the GPU stays the owner's call, as the proposal says.

**11. Smaller points.** Drop I and K from M5: T − I has replicated twice and continuity with K comes from the pilot on M4e's hosts, where T, K and K\* will share hosts and genomes. Defer T − K\*_S3 unless the owner wants the mechanism question now; it costs half again as much and its interpretation is already caveated. The five ensembles need no level in the model, but report per-ensemble means as a secondary and consider a stratified bootstrap. Report the probability of every reading under every scenario, not only "raises".

## The questions

1. **Estimand:** T − K\* as primary, built as Rr plus N2's autapses. Not T − K. Pilot K\* before the power calculation.
2. **Target:** 0.002 at 80%, anchored as a third of T − I and as the classifier's resolution limit. 0.001 is out of reach for the method, not only for the budget. Report 90% beside it.
3. **Unresolved:** the resolved-genome reading, plus "not fading" as a secondary, plus a qualifier when K\* has more unresolved genomes than T. Pilot the longer horizon on M4e's 70 genomes.
4. **Variance model:** fit on differences, three sources plus the pilot, independent arms, overdispersed host base rates, host component as a range.
5. **Scenarios and rule:** keep the scenarios; change the rule to minimum compute; include D = 1 and G = 512; redefine the guard and the envelope first; calibrate against M4e.
6. **Pre-registration:** yes. One round on the power output, then the plan.
7. **What else would make the answer wrong:** anchoring on K's base rate instead of Rr's, the 2^H envelope, the guard at 60 hosts, and reading a single-H answer off a variance that two experiments put a factor of ten apart.
