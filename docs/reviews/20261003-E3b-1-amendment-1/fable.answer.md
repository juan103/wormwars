**Verdict first: adopt with changes.** The amendment is the right instrument, the redraw is the right fix, and the text needs five additions. Everything below was checked against the files.

**1. Instrument and admissibility.** Yes on both. Without it, the registered rules fail deterministically: T-A crashes at run 1, generation 97, attempt 2 is "unchanged" by §5, crashes identically, and the stage goes final with all 8 runs "failed". The gate is then "not read" by design. A registered text that cannot execute needs an amendment. It is outcome-blind: feasibility is a property of the generator, no score exists, and the stopped attempt is recorded in `project.json`. One caveat the text should state: the feasibility enumeration read the test block's walls (ids 6000-6255), which §12 test 8 refuses before evaluation. No organism played, but say so.

**2. Redraw, not skip.** Both are rejection sampling from the feasible-maze distribution, so neither biases the mazes. Redraw keeps every registered id list and block size, keeps `train_ids` untouched, and needs no hook in `evolve_batch`. Skip changes registered ids and can collide within a generation's 16 draws. The attacks on redraw that the text must survive:
- The k = 0 stream must stay exactly `[run_seed, maze_id, 0x3A11]`. If k is appended to every draw, every maze changes. Say the redraw uses a distinct seed list for k ≥ 1.
- Bound k (say 64) and raise beyond it. At about 0.08% per draw, k = 2 has probability near 1e-6, but the loop should not be unbounded in principle.
- The placement stream stays `[run_seed, maze_id, episode, 0x9ACE]` on the final walls. That is already true of the proposal. Say it.
- "Something else": relaxing eligibility changes the task, and excluding ids is the skip. Neither is better.

**4. What else this exposes.**
