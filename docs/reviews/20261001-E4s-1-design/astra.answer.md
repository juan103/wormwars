**Verdict: revise.** Keep the basic experiment. The comparisons can answer whether starting evolution with L1 helps, but several interpretations in [design v1](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/docs/E4s/E4s-1-DESIGN.md) exceed what the measurements establish.

Must-fix items:

1. **“Outcomes”, O1/O1b — distinguish starting advantage from evolutionary improvement.** Keep both final-score comparisons. M−N at generation 999 is **not** a foregone manipulation check merely because G0 uses the module. Conversely, M−R can reflect harm from random signs without M outperforming N. Register descriptive F−G0 changes within each arm and differences between those changes; the necessary evaluations are already planned. Final superiority alone must not be described as improvement produced by evolution.

2. **“Measures”, turn offset — measure an offset, not just average behaviour.** `motor_stats` averages closed-loop commands, mixing intrinsic bias, sensory responses and visited trajectories. Add open-loop \(u(m,0)\) at the registered common-input levels alongside signed \(K_D\); retain forward command and saturation statistics too. Those are particularly relevant when half the random backgrounds drive backwards. I would **not** impose a turn-neuron bias in the main arms: that changes the host intervention and answers a different question.

3. **“Outcomes”, O2 — fix the historical labels and preserve the separate properties.** G0 and F are selected representatives, not necessarily ancestor and descendant. “Never used” cannot follow from two negative endpoints; “retained” cannot establish uninterrupted use. Even six checkpoints leave gaps. Report the D endpoint transition, Mc, and H transition separately, with labels explicitly describing endpoints. Rows 2–3 currently hide Mc/H distinctions behind their priority. Preserve all three E2d states: [its classifier](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/scripts/e2d.py:150) calls precise harmful effects “unclear” too, as C2 demonstrates. Report signed contrasts for every arm. C2 recovery should remain visible even when its G0 class prevents a clean transition label.

4. **“What E4s-0 changes” and O1b — remove three overclaims.** Approximately 5 is an empirical performance reference, not a proven score ceiling; the direct-path gain bound does not prove that exceeding this score requires another path. “R’s signs cannot flip … R cannot repair itself” is also wrong: [mutation is additive and cumulative](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/wormwars/brain.py:257), and compensation need not involve sign changes. Say sign reversal from ±3 is initially extremely unlikely at σ=0.02, then measure changes. Finally, 122/256 carrier mutants scoring below 0.7 establishes severe score loss, not functional lethality. Elitism preserves current eight-world winners, not guaranteed hold-out champions.

5. **“Gates” — retain the thresholds, specify the implementations.** Requalification and stopping on failure are appropriate. Define the state test as maximum absolute discrepancy under identical imposed input histories; otherwise closed-loop trajectory divergence confounds the numerical check. Cover validation’s **8 strains × 256 worlds**, which `evolve_batch` actually uses, alongside the stated training/evaluation shapes. Check the actual full-mask, zero-pinned N construction, not only `without_outputs`. The existing engine replay passed; that does not replace these pending graft checks. The “about 1%” failure probability should be labelled a plug-in estimate conditional on the pilot mean.

6. **“Engineering” — add explicit mutation-mask checks.** [The existing `module_scales`](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/wormwars/e4s/diagnostics.py:56) **pins the host**; passing it unchanged would implement the wrong experiment. Test host factors = 1, module factors = the arm’s value, N output factors = 0, and F0 module factors = 0. Restore the adopted plan’s mutation logging. Also distinguish R’s reset to its own initial random module from replacement by L1; the latter is a rescue intervention, not “reset-to-seed”.

7. **“Budget” — correct the evaluation rate and reserve.** The 80 runs, ten batches and approximately 14 training hours check out. But [E4s-0’s timing record](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/experiments/E4s-stereo-module/E4s-0/project.json:390) gives **306 episodes/s for 1 × 1,024**, versus 863 for **4 × 1,024**. The stated 2.46 million endpoint episodes therefore cost about **2.23 hours alone**, before trajectories, population probes and overhead. Measure every actual shape, including instrumented 1 × 256 probes, and freeze stage caps plus an evaluation reserve. **24 hours looks plausible; 16–17 hours is not adequately supported.**
