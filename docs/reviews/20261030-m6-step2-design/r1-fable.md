I've read the design note, M6-0's results, the brain's update rule, 02b's analysis code, the rollout and the task configuration. Here is my review.

**Verdict: proceed to a plan, with fixes.** The intervention is sound as a test of whether the champions' time scale matters. It is not a test of the history effect specifically, and the plan should say so in its title and its readings, not only in a confound paragraph. Two technical points need fixing before the run, and two cheap arms would make the result far more interpretable.

## 1. Is the intervention honest?

Yes, with three corrections.

- **The fixed-point claim holds, in both the continuous and the discrete system.** I checked the semi-implicit update in `wormwars/brain.py:13`. At a fixed point the time constant cancels, so the steady-state map is unchanged for any k. Good.
- **"Every transient is k times as long" is only true for the continuous system.** With substeps fixed at 32 and dt at 1/32, scaling tau by 0.25 quadruples c = dt/tau. That is a coarser discretization of the same flow, not an exact time rescaling. The exact version is to scale substeps by 1/k alongside tau: tau/4 with 128 substeps has the same per-substep c as the intact brain and simply takes four times as many of the intact brain's steps per world tick. k = 2 with 16 substeps is likewise exact. Either implement the arms that way, or run both implementations on three to five champions first and fix a tolerance in the plan. The cost of the exact version is roughly double overall, because the k = 0.25 arm costs four times the brain compute.
- **k < 1 leaves the evolvable space.** tau_min is 0.5, and nothing in `Brain` or the rollout clamps tau, so the run will work, but at k = 0.25 any neuron at the floor sits at 0.125, a value evolution could never have reached. This bears on the tuned-optimum argument. The plan should report the champions' tau distribution, in particular the share at tau_min and tau_max, before the run. If many neurons already sit at the floor, selection was pushing toward faster dynamics, and a drop at k < 1 becomes more surprising, not less.
- **State the prior.** T1 is single-nose foraging. With one sensor, climbing a gradient requires comparing the level now with the level a moment ago. Slow dynamics are therefore the mechanism of the task, and a drop at k < 1 is close to a foregone conclusion. The informative outcomes are the asymmetry against k > 1, the dose-response, and the comparison with T0 below. The plan should say in advance that a drop is the expected result and a null the surprising one, so a drop is not read as a discovery that "memory matters".

## 2. Controls

- **g00 and drift are weak as a generic-cost control, for a reason the note does not state.** They are untuned, low-scoring genomes. A genome that does little cannot lose much when its time scale changes, so a small drop in the controls is a floor effect, not evidence that the champions' drop is specific. Keep them, report their intact scores beside the champions', but do not hinge the primary reading on them.
- **The missing control is T0.** 02b found the same history effect in T0's champions, 0.452 against 0.281 for N2. T0 is stereo foraging, where gradient climbing does not need temporal comparison. If T0's champions drop as much as T1's at k = 0.25, the cost is generic to evolved controllers at this time scale, for example motor smoothing. If T1's drop is larger, that supports the single-nose memory account. 02b's records hold T0 g39 champions under M0, R1 and R2, about 78 genomes, and the blocks align by graph and run. Cost is under 20 minutes with tau-only arms. This is the one addition I would insist on.
- **Interior-only scaling, which the note asks about: add it on the champions.** Scale tau for every neuron except the read-out and sensory neurons at k = 0.25. If it costs as much as full scaling, motor and sensory latency are not the explanation. About six minutes. It does not resolve the latency confound for the interior, but it rules out its simplest form.
- **Symmetry needs k = 4, not k = 2.** k = 2 is log-symmetric with 0.5, not with 0.25. Add k = 4 on the champions so the dose-response reads 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4. Note the hazard at k > 1: every brain starts from v = 0, so the warm-up transient lengthens, and in a 200-tick episode that can cost on its own. The one champion's k = 2 result suggests it does not, but say it.
- **One drift replicate is thin.** Use M6-0's drift0 seed so the genome is one whose a and τ_stay are already recorded, and say that one replicate measures drift's response only coarsely. Two would cost 24 more minutes.
- **The validation on the probe checks the implementation, not the hypothesis.** For a time rescaling, a and τ_stay shrink by construction. Fix numeric criteria, for example τ_stay(0.25) below τ_stay(1) for at least 95 of 102 champions and a median ratio inside a stated band. "Minutes" is optimistic: M6-0's first pass took about 1.5 s per genome, so 306 genomes on three arms is nearer 25 minutes.
- **The dose-response is confounded** by the intact score and by each champion's overall tau level. Report Spearman of the drop against a, and against the median tau, with the cluster bootstrap over blocks.

## 3. The primary contrast and wording

The contrast as written has two problems. Relative change on a low-scoring control is ill-conditioned, and pooling g00 with a drift genome derived from it averages two correlated numbers. I would make the primary the champions' own paired relative drop at k = 0.25, averaged over the 30 blocks with the cluster bootstrap, read only if validation passes. Make the controls' drops, the k > 1 arms, the dose-response, the T0 comparison and the interior-only arm named secondaries with their own fixed wording. If the subtraction stays, use a common denominator, the champion's intact score, and say that a floor in the controls biases it toward specificity.

Suggested wording. Positive: "The champions' T1 foraging falls when every neuron is made four times faster, and not when every neuron is made slower. Their slow dynamics matter for the task. Whether that is memory of the food level or response latency this intervention cannot separate." Null: "The champions' foraging does not fall beyond X when every neuron is made four times faster. On these worlds their slow dynamics, the history effect included, are not needed." Fix X in advance. Also fix now: the batch composition, the check that every k = 1 score matches 02b's committed intact score for the 24 M0 champions, and the handling of blocks where a mapping's genome is missing.

Drop the power claim. M6-0's block-to-block spread was of a switch indicator and a rank share, not of a score drop. Nothing here supports "10% should be visible". Label the whole step exploratory and descriptive, as M6-0 was.

## 4. Cost, and anything not worth it

Nothing in the draft is worth cutting. With my additions and tau-only arms the total is about 2 CPU hours; with exact substep matching about 3.5. Set the cap at 5, which is what the owner was told. The reset and scramble alternatives are rightly rejected. A tau cap, setting every tau above some ceiling to that ceiling, would target the slow neurons alone and stay inside the evolvable space, but it changes relative timing and does not touch slow modes that arise from recurrence. I would not add it now.

## 5. Verdict

Proceed to a plan. Fixes in order of weight: retitle and reframe the claim as time scale, not history effect, and state the prior; fix the discretization by matching substeps or by a pre-run check with a tolerance; add the T0 champions, k = 4 and the interior-only arm; make the champions' own paired drop the primary and the control subtraction a secondary; set numeric validation criteria; report the tau distribution; remove the power sentence.
