**Verdict: go to v2 with these changes.** The task is fair as a "transfer necessary" task once two engine rules are added. Two findings are blocking for v2, because one can make the positive control fail for geometric reasons and the other can desynchronise the latch from the world.

**Q1, the X0 paths.** I checked each against the code. B's own senses: the sum field is the same under either draw, so it carries nothing. Neural memory: the grafted modules have no gap junctions and short time constants, the carrier is silent, and the latch holds one bit that is the mode. Position: every B-leg starts at A, whose location is independent of the draw. Walls and decoy placement: fixed per maze, the draw keyed by episode, so nothing to learn. Peers: no trails, crowding off, wey to wey collisions do not exist, and the exposure read is zero under access none. Engine: A's only edges out of the module are the 16 comparator to motor edges, exactly the gated set, as §6.5 says. The task is fair conditional on findings 1, 2 and 5 below.

1. **Critical. The cue is out of range at the choice point.** The scent is a Gaussian of path distance in grid cells, not maze cells: `free_distance` is a breadth-first search over the 4c+1 grid, with sigma 3 and reach 9 grid cells. One maze cell is 4 grid cells.

   | Quantity | Value |
   |---|---|
   | Scent reach | 9 grid cells, about 2.25 maze cells |
   | §2 minimum B to B′ separation | 3 maze cells, 12 grid cells |
   | Real-B scent at a midway branch point, 6 grid cells out | e^-2 of peak |
   | Follower, trails off, E3f 6×6 | 3.05 visits per wey against the walk's 1.75 |

   So for most of a B-leg A's channel reads zero and the wey is a wall-reflex random walk. It climbs whichever patch it bumps into first, and once inside the decoy's patch B's own sum pulls it in while the cue is still zero. The hand control can then fail with nothing wrong in the architecture, and §5 would void E4's null rows on that failure. Fix: state the units; require the last common cell of the tree paths A to B and A to B′ to lie within scent reach of both candidates, or raise E4-D's reach and sigma as a declared difference from E4's world; and compute the cue's lateral gradient at the branch point over the block before anything is played.

2. **Critical. The relays must read zero at the decoy, and this rule is missing from §4.** The latch flips on the sensed level at_b through E3_RB, not on the world's goal. If the decoy's block raises at_b, Q flips to mode A while the world goal stays B. The organism then heads for A, earns no visit there, and at_a flips it back. Fix: at_a and at_b are the real sources' blocks only; a test, and a sabotage where the decoy raises at_b and Q flips.

3. **Important. The chance level is one half by randomisation, not by placement.** Given X0 is cue-invariant, which the bitwise test shows, its real-first share is exactly one half in expectation whatever the geometry, because the draw is independent of the walls. §3 misattributes this to §2. Placement symmetry matters for two other things: the control must overcome B's own pull toward the nearer candidate, and the "neither by H" rate. Fix the wording, and make the per-wey first-leg share the primary first-entry readout, with the per-leg share beside it, since a wey stuck at the decoy contributes one leg and a free wey many.

4. **Important. The decoy is a trap.** Under the sum channel a wey inside the decoy's dead end sits at a local maximum of its goal scent with no event to release it, so it may stay for the rest of H. X0's visits become bimodal, and the control's gain in visits is mostly the stuck fraction. State it, report the stuck share and the censored legs, and name the rejected alternative, B's noses reading nothing in mode B, with the reason the sum is kept.

5. **Important. The one-tick window between the world's goal and the latch's sign.** D247 records that E-G's switches follow visits by exactly one tick. For that tick after a visit at A, A's noses already read the real-B scent while A's motor edges are still live. It is harmless only because A is at least ⌈c/2⌉+1 maze cells from B, far beyond the reach, and only if that distance check is also applied to A and B′. Write the structural argument into §3 rather than letting the empirical invariance test carry it, and note that "bitwise" holds within one batch composition, as D082 requires.
