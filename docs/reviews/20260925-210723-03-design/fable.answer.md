Review complete. Verdict first: the design is in good shape on the gate logic and the budget arithmetic, but it has one wrong factual conclusion (the throughput plateau is a chunking artefact), one ensemble that as written will fail or silently keep N2's structure (SH-mirror), two redundant signals, and a missing nuisance control (per-graph drive calibration) that decides every fitness signal.

**Q1. Ensembles and samplers**

1. MAJOR. SH-mirror as written is either unsampleable or silently keeps N2's asymmetric edges (`DESIGN.md:35-40`). Applying a swap "together with its mirror image where that image exists" has three cases. Both edges mirrored: fine. Neither mirrored: the lone swap can create a new mirrored pair by accident. One mirrored, one not: the lone swap orphans the mirror partner, and the share drops by two edges. Over 20 passes the share drifts toward the chance level of about 0.15 seen in ordinary shuffles, so an end-state acceptance band of 0.64 ± 0.03 has acceptance near zero. The opposite reading, swap only when both mirrors exist, leaves the 36% asymmetric edges untouched, which is N2 verbatim, and the Jaccard gate at 0.5 would still pass. Fix: partition N2's edges into mirror orbits (paired, self-mirrored, unpaired). Paired swaps within the paired set preserve degrees and symmetry exactly. Ordinary swaps within the unpaired set, rejecting any new edge whose mirror is present. The share then equals N2's exactly and the band is unnecessary. Collisions (a swap whose mirror is itself, edges between midline neurons) need explicit handling and tests.

2. MAJOR. The routing cap controls edge counts, but the weights are permuted afterwards (`DESIGN.md:33-35`, `graphs.py:97`). N2's permitted direct edges (AWCL to AVAL and SMDVL, weight 1 each) can receive weight 174 from FLP's pool. D036's rule is on weight (`remaps.py:29`), and that is what 02 blamed. Either keep the food-to-read-out edges' weights out of the permutation pool, or re-draw the permutation until the six D036 features are within N2's tolerance. Report the post-permutation direct weight as a gate, not only descriptively (`DESIGN.md:53-55`).

3. MAJOR. SH-route caps M0's neurons only, so within that ensemble R1 and R2 keep uncapped shortcuts. Signal (b)/(c) inside SH-route then compares a capped mapping with uncapped ones and the mapping preference flips sign for a generic reason. The cap must apply to every input pair used for fitness (M0, R1, R2) at each pair's N2 value, and to the MR neurons for the MR input response.

4. MAJOR. "Neuron class" is undefined and the two readings are far apart (`DESIGN.md:40-43`). The loader has five coarse types (`loader.py:21`, `fetch_connectome.py:39-43`); the roadmap's "swaps within neuron-class blocks" reads as the roughly 118 anatomical classes, which are not in the data. With anatomical classes most blocks hold one to four edges, so swaps are rare, the Jaccard to N2 stays far above 0.5, and the ensemble fails the mixing gate. With five types it is a block-model null, useful but weak. Also the condition is wrong as stated: block counts are preserved when target classes match *or* source classes match, not both. Requiring both over-restricts.

5. MAJOR. The current sampler fails silently. `_swap_chemical` and `_swap_gap` stop after 40× target attempts and return whatever was reached, with no count returned (`graphs.py:38`, `graphs.py:64`). Under routing, mirror and class rejection this is the likely path. No-op swaps (same source or same target) are counted as accepted (`graphs.py:43-52`). The sampler must return accepted and attempted counts, and the plateau rule (`DESIGN.md:48-49`) must be operational: run 2× passes and require the Jaccard to N2 and the mirror share to move by less than a registered amount.

6. MINOR. Jaccard at most 0.5 is far too loose. A well-mixed configuration-model shuffle of a 3709-edge graph on 302 nodes lands near 0.1 to 0.2. Set the gate relative to each ensemble's own plateau. Also record reciprocity and autapse counts per graph; both are generic properties the shuffles destroy, like symmetry.

7. MINOR. The curated left-right file must be the one used by both the sampler and the validator; `structure.py:18-26` still uses name suffixes. Commit its hash before building.

**Q2. Task C and the avoidance argument**

8. MINOR. The sign-flip argument is loose (`DESIGN.md:75-78`). The flip negates the food *input*; it maps approach to "approach of negative food", not to avoidance, and 02 showed it is exact only with gaps off while 03 runs gaps on. The conclusion survives on a different ground: with unselected genomes no graph has a mean valence, and "avoid food" is trivially solved by not moving. Say that instead.

9. MAJOR. Nothing in the design mentions motor gain calibration. 02 calibrated every graph in-world on 2048 genomes (`PREREGISTRATION.md:58`), because random-genome drive differs by graph and N2 is among the strongest drivers (`calibration.py:23-28`). Coverage in C is almost a direct function of drive, and gen-0 foraging is mostly drive too, so (b), (c), (d) are confounded until the pre-registration decides: per-graph calibration (258 graphs, about 1 to 1.5 GPU-hours, missing from the budget; MR needs its own turn calibration; N2's calibration is an N2 run and belongs after the pre-registration) or fixed gains with drive reported as a covariate.

10. MINOR. C is under-specified: hazards remain on (`config.py:107`, `world.py:540`) and become the only death route without drain, so C is partly hazard avoidance; the scripted coverage reference does not exist (`scripted.py` is all food-driven); "distinct cells the swarm visits" is a union or a sum; and 20 weys × 200 ticks × 0.35 cells can approach the 480-cell arena, so a ceiling compresses the z-scores.

11. Better control. Two options, both cheaper and better matched than C. First, T1 with `food_probe="constant"` (`world.py:504`): same world, same score, same drain, only the information removed. That isolates the food-driven part of N2's standing and shares drive exactly. Second, coverage computed from the same T0/T1 rollouts as a second score: zero cost, identical trajectories. C removes the food *input* too, which changes random-brain dynamics (N2's common-mode response is higher), so C confounds "task" with "input present". Run constant-food as the primary (d) contrast and keep C descriptive.

**Q3. Signals, gate, unit of analysis**

12. MAJOR. (b) and (c) are the same signal (`DESIGN.md:98-99`). If A is fitness minus the ensemble mean per mapping, then b_g = c_g minus a constant shared by every graph, so N2's percentile and N2 minus the mean are identical for both. The distinction the design wants ("N2 prefers M0" versus "shuffles are worse under M0") is the ensemble's own mean preference μ_E(c) with its interval. That is an ensemble property, compared with zero and across ensembles (prediction: SH-route's μ(c) is nearer zero than SH's). Register it as such; it does not fit the N2-versus-ensemble gate.

13. MAJOR. Only R1 and R2 for fitness contradicts 02's own conclusion that two remaps are too few because they disagree on T0 (`02-screening/RESULTS.md:263-268`) and the roadmap's "four remaps" (`ROADMAP.md:61`). Adding R3 and R4 by the D036 rule costs about 1.9 GPU-hours and still fits under the cap.

14. MAJOR. (a) absolute, (e), and the common-mode response are collinear through overall input sensitivity, which 02 found is what N2 actually has (`02-screening/RESULTS.md:168-172`). Register one sensitivity signal (common mode) and define the others as ratios to it: (a) as the ratio of per-graph means, signed and absolute separately, and (e) as 02b's fraction of the steady-state contrast, not a raw read-out. Also the signed (a) is the only M0-specific finding from 02, so keep signed and absolute as separate registered lines.

15. MAJOR. Multiplicity and no primary. Five signals × four ensembles × two tasks × several mappings is dozens of gates; at the 5% rank level expect one or two spurious stand-outs. Declare a primary set (signed (a) under M0, (c) on T1, (d) on T1, (e)), gate N2-specific claims on it, and label the rest exploratory. Register the expected sign from 02 for each so a reversed stand-out is reported as a reversal.

16. MAJOR. Four worlds (`DESIGN.md:87-88`). Every claim is then conditional on four maps. Worlds are free relative to genomes at the measured rate (the 256×8 row runs at the same throughput), so use 16 or more worlds and fewer genomes, fixed ids disjoint from 02's pools, shared across all graphs and tasks, with identical genome seeds across tasks so (d) is paired.

17. MAJOR. Attribution gap. Every ensemble has permuted weights; N2 alone keeps anatomy on its edges. A fitness signal that stands out from every ensemble cannot be attributed to topology versus weight placement. 02's N2perm control exists for this (`grid.py:174-179`); adding three permuted N2 seeds to the fitness rows costs about two minutes.

18. MINOR. The percentile rule needs the exact rank statistic. The exchangeability p-value is (r+1)/65; two-sided 5% then requires N2 beyond all 64 members, while "beyond the empirical central 95%" allows one or two. State which, and the interpolation.

19. MINOR. The mean over unselected genomes drops the head-start statistic of 01b and 02 (best of 32). Add a top-decile mean per graph as a registered secondary; it is a distribution summary, not selection, and it lets 03 connect to the earlier signals. Margins "from 02's effect sizes" are best-of-32 numbers; derive fitness margins from a shuffle-only pilot of the unselected statistic instead.

20. MINOR. N2-rev is reported but not folded into the verdict. If N2-rev shares a stand-out, the property belongs to the undirected wiring, and the verdict should say so.

**Q4. Budget and the 03a consequence**

21. MAJOR. The "saturation at 155 from 256 genomes upward" is a chunking artefact, not a GPU limit. `timing.py:38` calls `rollout` without `chunk_worlds`, so `cfg.evo.chunk_worlds = 512` applies (`default.yaml:119`, `rollout.py:107-108`). With 4 worlds that is 128 strains per chunk: the 256, 512 and 1024 rows all ran 512 worlds at a time in sequence, and the 256×8 row ran 64 strains per chunk. Throughput was still rising from 256 worlds (105 to 120 per s) to 512 (153 per s); nothing wider was measured, and the OOM branch never fired. 6.7 s for 6400 brain substeps is about 1 ms per step, which is launch-overhead territory, so wider chunks may scale well. Rerun with chunk_worlds at 2048, 4096 and 8192 before any conclusion. The 03a paragraph (`DESIGN.md:136-140`) should be withdrawn until then. The 5.5 GPU-hour arithmetic itself checks out; add calibration and the extra remaps.
