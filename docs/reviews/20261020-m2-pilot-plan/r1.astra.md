**Revise.** Findings ranked by importance:

1. **The fixed-point claim overreaches.** Against `Brain.step`, H is correct for the unmodified brain: M is `W_drive`, and gap row sums equal column sums because G is symmetric. The spike’s transpose and τ scaling are correct. But H=0 characterizes **substep** fixed points; tick-map fixed points can additionally include substep cycles whose period divides the substep count. With column Jacobian J_H and h=1/k, the equilibrium’s substep Jacobian is  
   `B = I + diag(h/(τ+h(1+g))) J_H`.  
   Check its spectral radius as well as continuous stability; validate against differentiation of `Brain.step`.

2. **“Verified exactly” and “reaches” are too strong.** Residual below 10⁻¹² establishes numerical root accuracy only subject to conditioning. Being within 10⁻³ does not establish that the trajectory converges to that root, particularly near basin boundaries. Calibrate proximity against continued float64 trajectories; record conditioning/Newton corrections, stability margins, and threshold sensitivity. Treat near-zero eigenvalues and uncertain root identities as unresolved. The 10⁻⁶ clustering threshold needs this error budget.

3. **R4 confounds slow convergence with instability.** A stable rate of −0.001 leaves a 10⁻³ perturbation around 6×10⁻⁴ after 500 ticks, failing the proposed test. Finite perturbations can also leave a stable equilibrium’s basin. Use progressively smaller perturbations, include leading eigendirections, and choose horizons using discrete decay rates. Random return trials support local stability; they do not prove agreement with an eigenvalue sign.

4. **R5’s continuum inference is invalid.** Five distinct equilibria can be five isolated attractors. Noncontiguous basin intersections along this history family are also compatible with discrete attractors. Nine starts provide a lower bound on accessible states, not their total number. A continuum requires additional evidence such as continuation and neutral tangent directions; ordinary Newton may fail precisely there.

5. **The oscillation rule is insufficient.** One near-return can occur during damped oscillation or an irregular transient. Require repeated returns across several cycles and persistent amplitude across windows. Inspect substeps for aliasing. Longer-period, quasiperiodic, and ambiguous cases should remain unresolved.

6. **The sample is reasonable for debugging, with two concrete provenance problems.** `growers.py` uses **512 genomes, seed 7602**, so seed-7600 diagnostics do not classify M1’s actual growers. Include a targeted grower stratum or explicitly call this replication. Also, the spike generates 64 genomes directly: because `Genome.random` draws parameters sequentially in batches, these are **not** a subset of the 1024-genome panel. Generate 1024 then select. Report carrier coverage after the cap.

7. **R1–R3 need signal-weighted checks.** Eventual verification does not validate equilibrium status at tick 300. Report contemporaneous agreement. Five percent unresolved or integration-discordant genomes could carry most of the numerator; report their tick-30/300 contributions and resulting uncertainty in the census split. R3 should include batch-composition changes and both separate-state subclasses.

8. **The fading-rate comparison omits excitation.** Read-out visibility alone is insufficient: the history difference must excite the mode through its left eigenvector. Compare discrete rates and fitted windows above the numerical floor; handle complex modes and cancellations explicitly.
