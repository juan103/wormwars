**Verdict: revise.** The fixed-point argument is correct; what needs fixing is one overstatement about the tick map, one rule the main record cannot evaluate, and one threshold that will produce false failures.

**Derivation, checked against `Brain.step`.** The substep is exactly v ← (v + c(d + tanh(v)M + vG))/(1 + c(1+g)) with M = W_drive (pre-by-post, row-vector convention), G symmetric, g its row sums. Cancelling c gives H(v) = 0; τ and dt drop out. The spike's Jacobian A[i,j] = sech²(v_i)M_ij + G_ij − δ_ij(1+g_j), its solve on Aᵀ, and the continuous stability from diag(1/τ)Aᵀ are all right. The spike's output matches the plan's "facts used". Note that M1's probe uses no silence mask, clamp or output gate; the plan should say so, since any of them changes H.

1. **"The equilibria of the tick map are those of the continuous system" is false as written.** The tick map is the substep map composed 32 times, so its fixed points also include period-k substep orbits for k | 32. Linearising the substep map at a zero of H gives P = I + dt·diag(1/(τ + dt(1+g)))·Aᵀ, i.e. explicit Euler on the chemical and off-diagonal gap terms with τ_eff = τ + dt(1+g). A flip (eigenvalue of P through −1) needs a real eigenvalue below −64 at dt = 1/32; with w_max = 3, τ_min = 0.5 and N2's hub in-degrees that is reachable where sech² is not small. Such a genome would show a tick-level range of 0 and Newton would land on an unstable zero of H, so it falls to "unresolved" by accident. Fix: state the claim for the substep map only, compute P's spectral radius at every verified equilibrium beside the continuous sign, and classify stability by P (what the census's integrator actually does). Disagreement between the two is then a reported quantity, and R4 becomes a nonlinear check of P rather than the only guard.

2. **The oscillation rule cannot be computed from the main record.** It needs lags of 2-100 ticks at tick resolution around tick 2000, but the record keeps every 10th tick after 300, and the rerun is triggered by the rule itself. Record every tick of 1901-2000 for all genomes (about 100 MB locally), or define the rule on the 10-tick samples and let the rerun refine it. Also, cycles longer than 100 ticks and quasi-periodic trajectories go to "unresolved" silently; say so.

3. **R4's return threshold ignores slow modes.** A stable equilibrium with slowest rate −0.005/tick leaves a 10⁻² perturbation at about 10⁻³ after 500 ticks, failing "below 10⁻⁶" and falsely contradicting the eigenvalue. Make the criterion relative to the prediction, e.g. final distance below max(10⁻⁶, 10·δ·e^{Re λ_max·500}), or integrate until the linear forecast is below 10⁻⁶.

4. **No planted controls.** Rule 9 asks for a sabotage check. Add two or three constructed genomes on N2's mask with known classes (a strongly reciprocal pair for bistability, an asymmetric pair for a cycle, a weakly coupled one for fading) and require the classifier to name them correctly.

5. **R5's "varies smoothly with the start" is not operational.** With nine points, use a count rule only (more than 4 distinct equilibria). A true continuum is non-generic for this ODE; what R5 detects is near-degenerate slow fading, as the plan half says.

6. **Minor.** Use damped Newton or report non-convergence explicitly from tick-300 and R5 starts, which are farther from equilibria than the spike's. R4's cost in float64 is likely 20-30 minutes, not 10; harmless. The classifier's "verified" could add the float32 residual |H| as a second reading, cheap and precision-aware.

The sample (5 graphs, carriers, cohort, 32 controls) is adequate for validating a classifier; representativeness is the census's job, as the plan states.
