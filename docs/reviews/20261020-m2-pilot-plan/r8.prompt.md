Answers to round 7:
- The damped spiral starts at amplitude 0.005, with the integrator's damping bisected to 2 × 10⁻⁶ a tick. R6 requires,
  for it, a period found, at least 90% of the range kept in every candidate history, the veto's reason, and that the
  same evidence without the veto (`classify.oscillation(..., ["unstable"])`) is a persistent oscillation (Fable 1,
  Astra 1). In a scratch rerun of stage r6, all six planted genomes were classified exactly; the spiral's period was
  found (473 substeps, 14.8 ticks) with 0.939 and 0.943 of its range kept and both orbit roots marginal, so the veto
  alone rejected it.
- **A design change that came out of that rerun:** with the spiral rebuilt, its period was first missed, because the
  candidate rerun recorded at 64 substeps while the integrator being classified is 32: at 64 the explicit term adds
  less growth, and this near-marginal spiral decays by about 2% a period there. The rerun now records at the substep
  count being classified (32, or 64 in R3's variant), like the continuation and the veto; at 32 a cycle of 2 ticks or
  more has at least 64 samples a period. The plan says so.
- Summaries added: the stall at tick 2000 (only where |δy| is constant over 1801-2000) against 0.1 a, with |δy(300)|
  against 0.1 a reported apart, and both counted within M1's cohort (Astra 3, Fable 2); the cheaper "distance alone"
  rule against the full verdict, per history, as a 2 × 2 count (Fable 2).
- An inaccurate root (error above 10⁻⁹, including an infinite bound) is "not tested (inaccurate root)" and is not
  continued (Fable 3).
- R3: a separate-state history keeps its root only if, in the variant, it reaches an accurate, stable root within
  10⁻⁶ of the main run's; a basin change with an unchanged class counts in the flagged numerator (Astra 2).
- "Just under 50 ticks" (Fable 4).
