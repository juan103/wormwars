**Verdict: Proposal 1 agree (one cheap addition). Proposal 2 revise: right slice, wrong controls as written.**

I checked M1's Part C, the census results and plan, and the census script. The Part C numbers quoted are as filed, the per-genome roots are stored locally in the census `.npz` files, so a "same root" reading at tick 300 needs no new root-finding.

**Proposal 1, ranked**

1. **Add batch 7's six arms to the predictor table.** It costs seconds and is the only contrast where C1, C1′, C2 can earn or lose credit: the core-frozen shuffles keep N2's separate states and retention, the hub-frozen mostly do not. If the predictors do not separate those two arms, say so in the M3 note. Correlations over 30 reference graphs with 19 zero counts will say little.
2. **Name what C3 is.** M1-PLAN defines C3 from N2's K, and "C3-kept fraction" is N2-relative. It is a core from N2's anatomy, not a per-graph anatomy rule. The note should also say plainly that the within-ensemble test never could test N2's handle, since N2 is outside the nulls' range on all three measures. That is a scope limit, not a null.

**Proposal 2, ranked**

1. **The read-only control is vacuous as written.** A cut at the reading tick alone changes one tick of a system whose late rate is about 0.04 per tick. "Hold beats read-only" would be guaranteed. Use an equal-duration cut ending at the read (ticks 11 to 30). Note too that the eight read-out neurons lie inside C3, so "C3's internal edges" includes the read-out's own afferents.
2. **Controls matched on count and summed |w| only are weak, and the roadmap promised more.** C3 is by construction the densest 30-set, so no coherent matched set exists; scattered external edges are a different kind of perturbation. Keep the anatomical control, state the pool is external-only (both endpoints outside C3, boundary edges excluded), and add an effect-matched control: scale the external cut until the first-ticks drop in ‖δv‖ matches C3's. "Store" should have to beat both.
3. **H = 20 may be too short.** If ‖δv‖ inside C3 has not collapsed by tick H under the cut, "no collapse after restoration" means nothing. Require that diagnostic, and run two H values.
4. **Cross-graph comparability.** In a reference graph, N2's C3 neurons enclose a different, smaller edge set. Define what is cut there and report its size; "N2's lead with and without the hold cut" is not a like-for-like cut.
5. **Power.** N2 has 28 separate-states genomes, the reference 38. Add the selected graphs' 387 for the class question; their states live off the core, a free location contrast.
6. **Make ‖δv‖ inside versus outside C3 the primary trace.** Store: inside collapses and does not regrow after restoration. Access: it regrows. Define "beats" with a margin and bootstrap per class before running.
7. **Gap-off and autapses.** M1 found gaps off lengthens memory, so keep arm (c) descriptive, outside the rule. Say whether core autapses are cut (C3 was defined without them).
