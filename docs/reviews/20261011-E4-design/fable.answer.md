**Verdict: revise.** The design is close to the roadmap's list, but three things undercut the readings as written: the modules are not isolated by the gating, so the primary contrast's "modes" and the diagnostic's necessity argument both leak; T1 cannot be powered within the ceiling at E3c's observed run-to-run spread; and T2's matched control is structurally biased toward a null. Each is fixable without GPU time, and two of the fixes are measurements that can run on the CPU now.

## The five questions

**1. Does it answer the roadmap's question?** Partly. A positive T1 would be interpretable as "the option of cross-links raised the attainable level", because mutation load can only hurt X+. A null T1 is not interpretable, for four reasons the design does not name: T1's minimum detectable effect is about twice the registered margin at any affordable R (see question 4); X+ carries 32 extra mutable scalars perturbed every generation, a load that can mask a benefit; the cross-links start at 0 and move at σ 0.02 per generation under the T-F factor, so a neutral slot drifts only to about 0.35 in 300 generations against within-module weights of 3; and X0 has alternative routes for the other module's information through the gate leak described under question 3. The named failure mode in §2 covers only "the interfaces block transfer". Also, the design should say what "transfer" can mean here. With comparators gated by saturation, the inactive module computes nothing, so the only information a cross-link can carry is the other module's raw nose signal. A positive result means "sensor sharing across modules helps", which is a real finding but a narrower one than "the two minds share". The partition under question 2 makes this explicit.

**2. The open decisions in §8.**

- **Mode clamp:** drop it. `Brain.clamp` at `wormwars/brain.py:364` is per strain and constant over the rollout; a clamp "from the moment the latch switches" is per wey and time-varying, so it is an engine facility, not "an intervention on states". More important, it probes a modified organism: a champion that co-adapted to the leak behaves differently clamped, and attributing the clamped result to the unclamped champion is the error. If isolation is wanted, put it in the organism itself before training (see question 3).
- **Cross-link set:** both modules are 4 neurons (`experiments/E4s-stereo-module/E4s-0/module.json`), so "every directed A↔B edge" is exactly 32, not "32-50". Declare two subsets and read them separately in T2: 16 sensory links (a nose to the other module's comparators or noses) and 8 comparator links (a comparator to the other module's comparators). Links into noses duplicate links into comparators through one tanh and one τ; keep them or not, but say why. Initialise at 0: generation 0 of both arms is then numerically the E3f-validated seed, because `Brain` builds a dense W so an unused slot changes nothing. Make both arms share one extended connectome with the slots present, and give X0 scale 0 on them with `assert_frozen`. `graft_connectome` at `wormwars/graft.py:92` adds only listed synapses, so X0 as "the T-F mask" on the plain seed would be a different BrainSpec, a different edge order and a different mutation stream.
- **T2's matched control:** neither count nor summed |w| works. There are 47 within-module edges, hand-set at |3| and load-bearing by construction, against 32 cross-link slots; cutting a within-module link breaks the stereo comparison, so "cross cut costs more than matched within cut" is nearly guaranteed false whatever the cross-links carry. The roadmap's wording at `ROADMAP.md:426` inherited this. Use mean substitution instead: cut each cross-link and add its time-averaged drive, measured on validation mazes, to the target's bias. Cut minus mean-substituted isolates the informational part; mean-substituted minus sham isolates the bias part. It is a genome edit per champion, no engine change. Keep the |w|-rank-matched within-module cut as descriptive only. Also pre-register a "not recruited" branch: if every cross-link's |w| stays within the neutral-drift band, T2 reads "cross-links not recruited", not "no information".
- **Diagnostic task:** the hazard design's necessity claim fails on the current organism, because in X0 the hazard nose can drive A's comparators far enough out of saturation to push the motors in B mode through A's live output edges (question 3). Fix the gating first, then the argument holds. Randomise hazard placement per maze or X0 can avoid hazards by route habit. A zero-engine-work alternative worth weighing: a seed whose noses are swapped, so each module senses the other's scent. With the gate frozen and closed, X0 cannot navigate and X+ can only through sensory cross-links. Its cost is that generation 0 is broken, so it is a search positive-control rather than a naturalistic task. Either way E4-D should be its own pre-registration and its own ceiling, decided now, not carved from T1.
- **Trail-mediated transfer:** the X+ minus X0 contrast already holds trails constant, so trails are not a confound of the contrast. They confound the size reading: if the trails already carry what the other module could add, a null reflects redundancy with the world channel. Trails are per source, laid by weys that last visited that source, and sensed through the matching module's noses (`wormwars/e3/maze_world.py:12-15`). So in A mode, B's trail gradient along the shared corridor points back to B; a cross-link that descends it is a plausible useful link. Run T2 under shared, own and no trails; if the cut costs more with trails off, the world channel was substituting.
- **Arms' start:** from the seed only. E3f showed coverers score almost nothing on islands, so from-scratch arms would mostly sit at the floor. Pair runs across arms by run seed: `Genome.mutate` draws the same noise with or without scales (`wormwars/brain.py:263`), so with a shared connectome the X+ twin of an X0 run receives identical within-module mutations until selection diverges. It is free variance reduction; the pilot measures how much it buys.
- **Missing:** a pre-registered gating check on champions, since the Q edges and comparator biases are in the T-F mask through `_grafted` at `wormwars/e3/samplers.py:128` and evolution can un-gate; a defined gene-duplication measure, such as the A versus B parameter distance against its neutral expectation plus each module's K_D from the existing probe; a sabotage check for the T2 cut path, per rule 9; the eligibility read on validation, not test, since the test block is used once; the level for T1, one contrast now, not E3c's 0.025 for two; and §7's three citations concern how modularity arises, not transfer between fixed modules, so the comparison is loose until checked.

**3. What is wrong about the organism and gating.** The roadmap at `ROADMAP.md:422` assumes "module A's motor output is switched off during B mode". It is not, and the leak is large at the designed weights. From `wormwars/e3/organism.py:21-26`, the inactive comparators sit at an effective bias of about −3.83, and their noses still feed them at ±3. A nose current is sensed field times 0.35, and the trail field is linear with no clamp, so a heavily trailed cell with the other nose occluded by a wall gives the full one-sided drive:

| Case | Inactive comparator state | Output tanh | Motor drive vs the active module |
|---|---|---|---|
| no nose drive | −3.83 | −0.999 | 0 |
| full one-sided drive, gate ±2 | −0.83 | −0.68 | about 16% |
| full one-sided drive, gate ±4, bias −3.83 | −4.66 | −0.9998 | about 0.01% |

That leak pulls the wey toward the source it just left, is reducible in both arms by the mutable gate edges, and is reversible only in X+ through cross-links. It also means X0 is not "cross-links impossible" for the other module's sensory information. Three consequences: measure it on the CPU now from recorded states of the seed on a few E4 mazes; then either close it with a stronger gate and freeze the selector's 13 parameters in both arms, re-checking G3a and G3b on E4's mazes as §4's precondition, or keep it and state that modes are soft. §3's claim "in X0 it must not" is false under the leak: deleting a saturated A comparator in B mode changes a constant motor push. Reframe §3 as per-source-neuron cross-link bundle cuts in X+, with the X0 deletion matrix kept as a measurement of the leak path. Mode-conditional deletion is the same per-wey engine facility as the clamp; if it is not built, use whole-rollout deletions and read per-mode leg durations rather than leg counts, since B legs only start after A visits.

**4. What the pilot should measure, and the power analysis.** The relevant spread is E3c's P-joint arm, tuned from the seed as E4's arms are: 0.90 visits across 8 runs (`experiments/E3-ab-organism/E3c/RESULTS.md`, §7.1 table). The margin at E3f's seed mean is about 0.67 visits. At 80% power, two-sided 0.05:

| R per arm | Minimum detectable X+ − X0 |
|---|---|
| 8 | 1.26 visits |
| 10 | 1.13 visits |
| 20 | 0.80 visits |
| for the margin 0.67 | about 28 per arm |

E3c's ledger gives about 0.84 GPU-h per 300-generation run; at H 1 800 the training budget buys roughly 9 to 10 runs per arm. T1 therefore cannot resolve the margin and will read "unclear" for anything under a visit, as E3c's Q2 did. Make T2 the primary confirmatory reading, with the exact sign-flip test across champions on the mean-substituted contrast, and T1 secondary with its minimum detectable effect stated in the pre-registration. The pilot should measure, in order: the leak distribution on the seed, CPU only; per-generation timings at 6 × 6, H 1 800, to fix R and G; the between-run SD per arm and the correlation of seed-paired X0/X+ outcomes; the cross-link |w| distribution against neutral drift, at the T-F factor and at factor 1.0 on the cross-links only, to choose the scale; the mutational-load asymmetry as the generation-1 mean fitness of X+ against X0 under common random numbers; and the per-champion cut-versus-sham difference and its maze SD, which sizes T2. The power analysis then follows E3c's `power.json` pattern for T1 and adds a sign-flip simulation for T2.

**5. Verdict:** revise.

## What to change

1. Measure the gate leak on the CPU before anything else; decide between closing it with a stronger frozen gate or declaring soft modes. Record the decision.
2. Drop the mode clamp; reframe §3 as cross-link bundle cuts plus the X0 deletion matrix as a leak measurement, read on per-mode leg durations.
3. One shared extended connectome for both arms, 32 slots at 0, X0 frozen by scale with `assert_frozen`; a CPU test that the slotted seed reproduces the seed's per-maze visits exactly.
4. Partition the cross-links into sensory and comparator subsets and read T2 on each.
5. Replace the within-module matched cut with mean substitution; keep the matched cut descriptive; add the "not recruited" branch and a sabotage check.
6. Make T2 primary and T1 secondary; state T1's minimum detectable effect at the chosen R; fix the test level for one contrast.
7. Pair runs across arms by run seed; state the pairing as a variance reduction and let the pilot quantify it.
8. Pre-register a gating check on every champion and define the gene-duplication measure.
9. Decide the cross-link mutation scale from the pilot's drift measurement and justify it against the mutation-load asymmetry.
10. Make E4-D its own pre-registration and ceiling; fix the gating before relying on its necessity argument; compare the hazard task against the swapped-noses alternative.
11. Correct the small facts: exactly 32 edges, E3c trained 20 runs for about 16.9 h, eligibility read on validation, and §7's citations flagged as about modularity's origin until checked.
