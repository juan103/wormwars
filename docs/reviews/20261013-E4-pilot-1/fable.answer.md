**Recommendation.** Do not choose among A to D yet. Finding 2 is expected and matches E3b-1's own record at the same point in training. Finding 1 is the real anomaly, and it is not explained by "T-F's mutation strength": the same kernel on E + W2 did not do this in E3b-1. Spend a few CPU-minutes and about 10 GPU-minutes to find out whether E-G is a knife-edge organism or whether something in the gated training path is wrong. Then re-set the load bound relative to X0's own load, keep the kernel, run the formal training at 300 generations with populations saved at every checkpoint, and bring the owner only the questions in point 4.

1. **Finding 2 is not supported as stated.** E-G's per-maze SD is about 3.7 (E3f's 256-maze interval, E3c's seed check), so one strain's 8-maze mean has an SE of about 1.3, and the max over 32 such means is mostly noise. The clean statistic is the population mean, and it says: elites hold near 6, children sit near 2.8. That is exactly what the repository's precedent looks like at 100 generations. E3b-1's T-F used the same GA, kernel and factor on E + W2: at index 124 its champions were +0.07 of the seed's mean, about 0.4 visits, invisible at the pilot's noise; the gain came between 124 and 299 (S-gen +0.315; P-joint's index-124 champions 4.91 to 6.88 against P-fixed's 5.31, index-299 6.20 to 8.36). The design's compute section already assumed 300-generation runs; the pilot's 100 was a budget cut and could never have shown "improvement continuing near G".

2. **Finding 1 is the anomaly, and the question's framing hides it.** E3b-1's training table: T-F's population mean rose from 5.20 at generation 0 to 6.46 at the last generation, with 69 mutable parameters at these sigmas. E4's X0 has 56 mutable parameters (the pilot record), the same sigmas, and one mutation step of σ_w 0.02, σ_bias 0.0125 and τ × exp(0.0375) costs 4.7 visits (6.23 to 1.94, children alone about 1.5). The kernel did not change; the organism and the arena did. Three candidate causes: the hard output gate makes E-G's behaviour a switch with no graded neighbourhood; the 6 × 6 islands are brittle to small parameter changes; or a defect in the gated children's mutation or evaluation. The pilot cannot separate them. Until this is known, every option A to D is treating a symptom.

3. **The cheap checks** (all CPU or minutes of GPU, 32 children × 8 mazes each):
   - generation-1 load by parameter class: w only, τ only, bias only; module A only, B only; the gated comparator → motor edges only; and p_mutate set so one parameter changes per child, to find any lethal parameter;
   - a 2 × 2: E + W2 ungated on E4's 6 × 6 mazes, and E-G on E3b-1's 5 × 5 trees, same kernel. This separates gate from arena, and a large load in every cell points at a bug;
   - free, from the saved selection matrices: children-only mean per generation (exclude the 3 copied elites via the parents' child flags), to see whether robustness is being selected;
   - about 8 GPU-minutes: the 4 saved final X0 populations on the 64-maze validation block against E-G (128 strains × 64 mazes, about 8 training generations' work). SE about 0.4 per strain. This settles "has anything moved above E-G at 99" better than any best-of-8 reading.

4. **The options, as claims.**
   - **A** is premature. Uniform 0.05 to 0.1 slows adaptation 2.5 to 5 times when the precedent says 0.25 is survivable for E + W2. If the class-wise check finds one or two lethal classes, scale those alone. p_mutate < 1 is defensible but gives up the "as T-F" comparability that was the reason for the kernel.
   - **B** changes the question from "do co-evolving duplicated modules recruit a channel" to "can a fixed pair be wired to help itself". It also drops gene-duplication fate and the A-B distance reading. Worth adding as a third, cheap arm (32 parameters, no load problem, factor 4 admissible), not as a replacement. Replacing is the owner's call.
   - **C alone fails** for a reason the scale rule's own code admits: at factor 0.25 and 70 to 170 mutations on a 300-generation lineage, a neutral slot drifts to about 0.2, so the slots are frozen in effect and P2 and recruitment are null by construction. C needs the bound changed.
   - **The load bound is the real design defect.** 0.25 absolute presumed ordinary mutation costs little; it costs 4.3. D250 already sent the level to the reviewers, and the design says the rule is decided before the formal run, so a pilot-informed relative bound is in scope. Truncation selection is rank-based, so define it on children only: X+ children's generation-1 mean at least 0.8 (or 0.75) of X0 children's under common random numbers. At 0.8 that admits factor 1.0, whose reach on 120 mutations is about 0.25, so the rule picks 1.0 as reach-deficient; at 0.75 it admits 2.0, whose reach is about 0.57, and the rule picks 2.0 by its "reach and load" branch. Say which, and why, before the run.
   - **D:** more mazes or larger populations lost to more generations in E3b-1 (T-A against T-F at matched evaluations). A different seed removes the point of E4.

5. **The next pilot.** First the checks in point 3. If they show a defect, fix it and re-measure the generation-1 load before anything else. If they show E-G is intrinsically brittle, go to point 6 before spending GPU. Otherwise one batch of 8 runs, 4 X0 and 4 X+ paired, 100 generations, the cross factor by the relative rule, populations written to disk at every checkpoint. Expected cost about 1.3 to 1.6 GPU-hours. It must measure: seconds per generation at 8 runs per batch (E3c's ledger implies about 7 s per run-generation against the pilot's 14, so the 4-run batch probably underfilled the GPU); children-only generation-1 load; the slots' |w| trajectory against the simulated neutral walk; P1-cut on the validation champions with its maze SD; the cost of each intervention condition. Do not run a 300-generation pilot: the "does gain appear by 300" question is answered by E3b-1's precedent and will be answered properly by the formal run's own checkpoints, saved every 25 generations.

   Budget: 30 h minus 3 spent, 1.5 for this pilot and 3 to 5 for probes leaves about 20 h for training. 16 runs × 300 generations at the pilot's rate is 18.7 h linear, less if 16-run batches scale as E3c's did. If the throughput measurement says more than 20 h, the choices are R = 7 per arm (the sign-flip test's minimum), G = 250, or a higher ceiling.

6. **What the owner must decide.** Three things, and only if they arise: replacing co-evolution with link-only evolution (B as a replacement); raising the 30-hour ceiling or accepting R = 7 or G = 250 if throughput forces it; and the organism itself if the checks show the hard gate makes E-G a knife edge, because then E-G's evolvability is a property of the engineered switch, which bears on what "evolved organisms" means in E4's readings, and the fix (a graded gate) is a new organism. The relative load bound, class-wise mutation scales justified by the diagnostics, G = 300 as the design's compute section already assumed, and checkpoint saving are design details for the two reviewers, labelled pilot-informed.
