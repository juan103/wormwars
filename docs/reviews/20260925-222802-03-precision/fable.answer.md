**Verdict up front:** (a) does not close on its own arithmetic for P1, (b) rests on an SE that is probably overstated for P2 and P3, and (c) is right about the rank test but wrong to de-attenuate with that SE. The precision criterion itself targets the wrong verdict. Details, most important first.

## Major

1. **The precision SE is the wrong SE for P2 and P3, and likely overstated by up to about 1.7×.** `cmd_precision` (scripts/exp03.py:304-307) resamples one graph's genomes and worlds independently. Two problems. First, the registered interval (wormwars/exp03/verdict.py:74) shares world indices across N2 and every ensemble graph, so the world main effect of the T1 minus T1-const contrast cancels there but is counted in the pilot SE. Second, the product bootstrap on a crossed design overweights the residual term by a factor of about 3 (coefficient (2−1/G)(2−1/W)−1 ≈ 2.8 at 64×16), so if per-genome-world residual dominates, the SE is √3 too large. Between-graph SD is a real spread, not inflated, so reliability is underestimated and (c)'s de-attenuation subtracts too much. If the true P3 SE is nearer 0.007, reliability is about 0.8, not 0.5, and the demotion in (b) is premature. This is checkable now from pilot.json without any N2 run: fit a two-way decomposition (genome, world, residual) per pilot graph, and run the registered `effect_interval` with SH101 as N2 and the other 15 as the ensemble. Use all 16 stand-ins, not `graphs[0]` (scripts/exp03.py:302). P1 and P4 use a one-way bootstrap over probe genomes, so their SEs are fine.

2. **(a) fails its own criterion for P1.** At 8192 brains the SE is 0.0258/4 = 0.0065. The de-attenuated margin is 0.5 × 0.038 = 0.019, a quarter of which is 0.0048. Against the registered pilot margin (pilot.json:22) a quarter is 0.0058. Both fail. Passing needs about 15 000 brains (de-attenuated) or 10 000 (pilot margin). In reliability terms, 2048 brains already give ρ = 0.90 for P1 (from 0.69) at about 6.5 s per graph instead of 33 s. P4 at 2048 is right and passes (0.0065 < 0.009), but its reliability only moves from 0.97 to 0.99, so it buys almost no rank-test power.

3. **The binding constraint for "distinctive" is the rank test's discreteness, not the interval.** With n = 128, p = (r+1)/129, and Holm over four signals (verdict.py:30-39), the smallest-p signal needs p ≤ 0.0125, so r = 0 in all five ensembles: N2 beyond all 640 graphs. Steps two to four need r ≤ 1, 2, 5. Per-ensemble probability of r = 0 under a normal null, by N2's position in observed SDs:

   | z | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | P(r = 0 of 128) | 0.05 | 0.45 | 0.84 | 0.97 |

   Across five ensembles these roughly multiply. From 02's numbers (experiments/02-screening/RESULTS.md:163,171), N2's signed ratio at M0 is about 0.0029/0.030 ≈ 0.10, which is z ≈ 2.1 against the pilot's 0.046, or z ≈ 2.5 at 8192 brains. So P1 "distinctive" at step one is a coin flip per ensemble even at 8192. The pre-registration needs a simulated power table for the rank test at the chosen allocation. Note the hidden benefit of (b): with two primaries, Holm's thresholds become r ≤ 2 and r ≤ 5, which at z = 2.1 raises per-ensemble step-one power from about 0.10 to about 0.60. Say this explicitly.

4. **The compatibility criterion tests closeness to the ensemble mean, not membership** (DESIGN.md:242, verdict.py:51). A true member of E lies within ±0.5 SD of the mean only about 38% of the time. With SE at a quarter margin, the 90% interval half-width is about 0.25 SD, so "compatible" is reachable only when N2 sits within ~0.25 SD of the mean: about 20% of exchangeable N2s. Astra's 26% figure assumed N2 exactly at the mean, which is not the exchangeability null. Replace it with a rank-consistent rule: N2's 90% measurement interval lies inside the ensemble's central 90% (5th to 95th percentile of the 128 observed values). The precision criterion then becomes reliability: SE ≤ about 1/3 of the true between-graph SD (ρ ≥ 0.9). P4 passes at 512, P1 at 2048, P2 and P3 pending point 1.

5. **Margins from 16 graphs are too unstable to register as numbers.** An SD from n = 16 has about 18% relative SE; the variance about 37%; after de-attenuation where noise is half the variance, the residual has relative SE above 70%. Register the rule instead and compute the margin from each ensemble's own 128 graphs at run time, de-attenuated with the mean per-graph bootstrap variance across that ensemble. This uses no N2 data, so it stays pre-registered, and it fixes DESIGN.md:248 applying SH's SD to all five ensembles.

6. **The proposal exceeds the design's cap.** 645 × 76 s = 13.6 h plus 8.4 h plus the pilot and calibration validations is about 22.5 h against the 18 h cap at DESIGN.md:374. The pre-registration must restate the cap or cut.

7. **Better allocation, conditional on point 1.** P3 is the owner's question, and its pilot mean is −0.003 ± 0.0045: random shuffled brains show no food-information dependence, so the null spread may be mostly noise. For such a signal precision is power, because more samples shrink the null spread itself. P1 at 2048 (+6.5 s), P4 at 2048 (+10 s), and the four T1 fitness cells at 2× (+24 s) cost about what the proposal costs (+7.2 h) and spend it where it matters. To fit under 18 h, drop the T0-R1 and T0-R2 cells (−12 s) and run the six secondary response conditions at 256 brains (−7 s). A high-precision subset does not work: at n = 32 the minimum p is 0.03, which cannot pass Holm's first two steps. Measuring N2 alone at high precision keeps validity (conservative) but the ensemble's noise still sets the spread. Shrinkage with equal per-graph sample sizes is a monotone map, so ranks are unchanged. A random-effects decomposition is the right estimator of the true between-graph SD for margins and reporting, not a replacement test.
