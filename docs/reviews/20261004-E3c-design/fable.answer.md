**Verdict: revise.** The arm set and the task are right, but four decisions in v1 would make the run uninformative as drafted: the 0.25 mutation factor and 125-generation schedule cannot carry a from-scratch arm anywhere, the random 65-edge subset is a weak dense control, the init rule for the S arms is unspecified, and the cost ledger counts one tenth of the lineage. A v2 that takes the changes below can go to pre-registration.

## The central problem: the matching unit kills Q1 and Q2 before they run

The draft matches every trained arm to T-A: 125 generations × 16 mazes, with E3b-1's mutation factor 0.25 on 02's sigmas. I checked `wormwars/brain.py:257` and `wormwars/evo/evolve.py:67`: mutation is additive Gaussian, σ × factor, every child, every generation. So the per-generation step is 0.02 on an edge and 0.0125 on a bias.

The seed's working values are ±3 on nose and output edges (`E4s-0/module.json`), ±2 on the gate edges, −1.914 on the comparator biases, 2.0 on the latch self-edge. From a start near zero, a bias reaching −1.9 in 125 generations needs the selected parent to carry a 1.2σ move in the same direction every single generation. For 47 edges and 9 biases at once, that is not a search, it is a lottery. `docs/E3/DESIGN.md:362` already made this point for E3a's Stage 2 ("about 38 sigmas"), and that was at factor 1.0 over 300 generations, where 1 of 8 runs found a selector. The record you have for from-scratch training is E3a's B-task: factor 1.0, 800 generations, the open arena, and it reached 54 to 77% of E. E3b-1's R arm, the closest maze analogue (all 65 scalars from the zero-gate start, factor 0.25, 125 × 16), went from −0.92 to −0.56 of the seed.

Consequences as drafted:

- **S-mod and S-dense** will both sit near the floor. Q1 then becomes a Welch test between two floor values, which can still come out "significant" on noise. That is the "both fail" case §6 fears, and the pilot as drafted (2 runs × 25 generations at factor 0.25) cannot detect it, because 25 generations at σ 0.02 shows nothing either way.
- **P-sel** at factor 0.25 on 13 scalars cannot reach the seed's gate values in 125 generations by the arithmetic above. E3a's Stage 2 used factor 1.0 on exactly these 13 (`wormwars/e3/samplers.py:122`). Use 1.0 here too.
- **Q2** is foregone: P-joint starts at 5.84 visits and S-mod at or below W2 alone's 1.7. "Pretrained wins" is certain and tells the reader nothing the roadmap did not already warn about.

What to do instead:

- **Match on rollouts, not on the operator.** State that "equal training effort" means equal worlds × ticks, and that the from-scratch arms use factor 1.0 (the pilot confirms or adjusts it). Say openly that the mutation scale is then unequal to P-joint's, and why.
- **Match to T-F, not T-A.** T-F (300 × 8) costs 6.44 h against T-A's 5.12 and gives 2.4× the generations. Generations are what a from-scratch arm is short of, and T-F is the schedule that actually gained (+0.381 against +0.069). Reuse T-F's 8 final champions as P-joint. Budget: S-mod and S-dense at 8 × 300 × 8 (12.9 h), P-sel at 4 × 300 × 8 (about 3.8 h, by R's 42.7 s per generation at 1 024 worlds in `project.json`), ×1.25, plus pilot, project, g-e, champions and evaluation at the draft's figures, comes to about 25 h. P-sel is descriptive, so 4 runs is enough.
- **Make the cost curve the deliverable the roadmap asks for.** Checkpoint every 25 generations on one learning-curve block shared by every arm, and register a generations-to-threshold reading (first checkpoint whose generation-best exceeds W2 alone, and the seed's level, right-censored at 299). That is "first-use cost including pretraining", read directly. The equal-effort endpoint is one slice of it.

## §9's nine points

1. **The task: mazes.** Agree, with one caveat. The open arena is 30× cheaper, and B-task's 800 generations there still lost. If the pilot shows the floor in mazes, the cheap fallback is not shaping or an easier maze but running Q1 in the open arena at 8 × 800 × 16 for about 2.4 h per arm, where E3a already measured a dense arm. Name that fallback now so it is not a post-hoc choice.
2. **Reusing E3b-1's champions.** Sound, if three conditions are registered: `g-e` at E3c's commit passes E3b-1's three legs (the GPU hashes, the CPU equivalence, `maze-reference.json`); the champion files in `runs/e3b1/genomes` are verified against the hashes in `champions.json` at load; and the files are committed, since 8 champions are not "bulky" and an outsider cannot otherwise rebuild P-joint. The different validation block is harmless: it only chose which of 32 to freeze, under the same rule the new arms use. The real confound is not the commit but the schedule, as above. Reuse T-F's finals, not T-A's.
3. **The matched controller.** The random 65-edge subset is the weakest option. One draw gives one arbitrary topology, which may have no nose-to-comparator-to-turn path for a module and no recurrence at the latch position, so "modular wins" would be a straw man, and a reader cannot tell. Use B-task's full mask instead (`samplers.py:153`: all 121 edges among the 11 plus the 32 output edges, 171 scalars). It is a strict superset of E's mask. Then the readings are clean: if S-mod ≥ S-dense, the structural prior beats extra capacity under this GA; if S-dense > S-mod, capacity and structure are not separable, and you say so in advance. State the one confound the superset carries: more mutable scalars at the same σ means more noise per child. Keep σ equal and report it rather than invent a scaling rule. The roadmap's "matched size" means neurons; the draft's scalar matching is a departure that costs more than it buys.
   Keep the relays as the goal conditioning for Q1. But say plainly that the roadmap's arm 1 and D144's baseline ("one shared navigator plus a persistent goal bit") is the explicit goal-bit controller, that E3c does not run it because it changes the inputs, and that this is a departure.
4. **P-sel's start.** The degraded start is the right reading of "a second organism gets the modules and must find the selector", which is the cumulative reuse cost. Keep it, at factor 1.0, and write the expected outcome now: E3a's S2 found a selector in 1 of 8, R recovered to −0.56, so P-sel is likely to end below P-fixed. That is a finding about reuse cost, not a failure, and the wording should be fixed in advance.
5. **Equal training effort.** Equal rollouts, as above. Evaluation compute is the same for every arm and belongs in the ledger, not in the matching.
6. **The schedule.** 300 × 8, with T-F's champions as P-joint. §7 already sees this; make it the design.
7. **The cost accounting for hand-built modules.** The draft charges E4s-0's recorded hours. That record totals 814 s, which is 0.23 GPU-hours. It is not the pretraining cost of what P-joint starts from. The lineage of the seed E + W2, from the committed compute records:

   | Stage | GPU-hours | What it bought |
   |---|---|---|
   | E4s-0 | 0.23 | L1's calibration |
   | E4s-1 | 16.6 | L1's confirmatory validation |
   | E3a | 5.97 | the selector, E, its gates |
   | E3b-0 | 2.62 | W2 and the maze-ready additions |
   | E3b-1, the reused arm | 5.12 (T-A) or 6.44 (T-F) | the tuning |

   Register the ledger as a table with each line and let the reader choose the attribution. Give P-joint's first-use cost as a range, from the minimum (the reused arm plus E4s-0) to the full lineage of about 32 h plus the reused arm. W2's cost is common to every arm and cancels. Say that the hand-built lineage's real cost was design and review effort that GPU-hours do not measure. Counting 0.23 h would be the overclaim E3c's reviewers would catch first.
8. **The pilot's floor criterion.** Three changes. The floor is not zero: W2 is frozen in every arm and alone makes about 1.7 visits per wey, and a random controller with large outputs fights it and can score below. Define the floor as W2 alone on the carrier. Run the pilot at factor 1.0 for about 100 generations × 8 mazes with 2 to 4 runs per arm (about 1 to 1.5 h), and log the generation-0 turn offsets as E3a did. The criterion: at least one run of each arm whose generation-best on the learning-curve block exceeds W2 alone by a stated margin (1 visit is defensible from E3b-0's "+1.09 above W2 alone") by generation 100. If not met, the registered fallback is the open-arena Q1 from point 1, not shaping.
9. **The registered tests.** Q1: two-sample Welch on d over 8 runs each, with E3b-1's three labels (modular better, dense better, unclear, 5% each side), and a floor guard: the test is "not read, both at floor" unless at least one arm's test-block mean exceeds W2 alone by the pilot's margin. Beside it, the generations-to-threshold reading per arm, compared by the count of runs reaching the seed's level (Fisher's exact), with the median generation reported descriptively. Q2: a one-sided test is allowed but its informative content is the cost ledger; register the expected outcome ("P-joint better at equal effort") and make the ledger and S-mod's censored cost-to-seed-level the reported result. P-sel against P-fixed and P-joint: descriptive.

## Other findings

- **The S arms' init rule is missing, and it carries design knowledge.** "Random weights on E's mask" does not say the range, whether τ and biases are drawn, or whether E3a's tied antisymmetric draw is used (`samplers.py:167`). With independent signs, E's mask gives S-mod almost nothing of E's design, since the push-pull sign pattern is most of it; with the tied draw, S-mod inherits the sign pattern and "structure" means mask plus signs. Either is defensible. Pick one, apply the same rule to both S arms, and say what "structure" then means. The draft's "S-dense keeps E's τ and biases" while S-mod is random is an inconsistency to remove.
- **P-joint's existing learning curve was read on block 4500-4627 under E3b-1's code.** If the cost curves are compared, every arm's curve must be on one block. Either re-read P-joint's checkpoints on E3c's block (cheap; the T-F snapshots exist locally only at 124 and 299, so the curve would be two points) or compare curves only descriptively and say why.
- **"W2 alone" and "the seed" are the two reference lines every reading needs.** Both should be played on E3c's test block in the evaluation stage, with their test-block values the denominators, as E3b-1 did.
- **Draft numbers I checked and found correct:** T-A's 5.12 h and 143.5 s per generation (`project.json`, RESULTS), the 65 and 13 scalar counts (E3b-2 plan §4, `samplers.py:30`), B-task's −3.73, S2's 1 of 8, R's −0.92 to −0.56, and the §7 arithmetic (19.8 and 23.7).

**Verdict: revise.** Match on rollouts at factor 1.0 and T-F's schedule, reuse T-F's champions, replace the random subset with B-task's full mask, fix the init rule, define the floor as W2 alone with a guard, register the generations-to-threshold reading, and replace the 0.23 h with the lineage ledger. With those in v2, go to pre-registration.
