**1. Keeping E3d’s scientific settings is sound.**

- **Horizon:** keep H = 2,400. Increasing it to preserve coverage would work against the owner’s stated purpose. However, E3e §1’s claim that a larger maze “lengthens every route” and therefore navigator scores “will fall” is too strong. These are newly sampled mazes; longer typical routes and lower scores are expectations, not guarantees. [E3e §1](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/docs/E3/E3e-DESIGN.md:52)
- **Scent:** no mandatory rescaling. Corridor width, movement speed, and local sensing geometry remain unchanged. The cutoff remains nine **raster steps**, with the follower’s approximate detectable range of eight; it is not nine maze-cell hops. Longer journeys can increase discovery and trail-establishment costs. The inherited scent-reach splits and discovery records are therefore important. E3d already found substantially better performance with shared trails than with scent alone. [E3d §2](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/docs/E3/E3d-DESIGN.md:110), [world implementation](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/wormwars/e3/maze_world.py:248), [E3d results](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/experiments/E3-ab-organism/E3d/RESULTS.md)
- **G1b and G2b:** retain 2.0 and 4.0. These specify acceptable absolute throughput over the fixed horizon; they need not scale with maze area. Together with G1a, G2a, G3 and the oracle precondition, they prevent a nearly unsolvable larger maze from passing merely because blind scores collapse. A pass remains acceptance of this family on the specified block, not a size-independent claim about navigation. [E3d §5](D:/Claude/random/wormWars/docs/E3/E3d-DESIGN.md:212)
