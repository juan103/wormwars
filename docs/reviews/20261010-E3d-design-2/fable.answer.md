**2. The carved construction.**

- *Geometry:* correct. I checked the post and segment arithmetic in the script (lines 107-124) against `maze.py:89-94`: a vertical-neighbour segment between rows i and i+1 is the wall row 4(i+1), whose ends are posts (i+1, j) and (i+1, j+1), and the script's `touches` tests exactly that; likewise for horizontal neighbours. The excluded set (`g in s`) is exactly the goal's own four sides. After carving, each post's only wall neighbours are the goal's sides, and each side's perpendicular neighbours are open cells, so the ring is free-standing under 4-connectivity and under 8-connectivity. With Chebyshev distance at least 2 the two rings sit in disjoint closed blocks, so "no component touches both goal blocks" is automatically true for carved goals (a harmless redundant check).
- *A consequence the design does not state:* every one of the eight segments between consecutive cells of the goal's eight neighbours touches a goal post, so §2.3 opens all of them. Every goal therefore sits at the centre of an identical fully open 3 × 3 roundabout. The §2 claim that "the k_r random openings soften the stereotyped goal neighbourhood" is not right: k_r openings are drawn from all closed segments, and the roundabout's segments are already open, so they soften nothing near the goals unless they open a goal's own side.
- *The k_r pool includes the goals' own closed sides.* Script line 125 builds `closed` from every segment not yet open, which includes them. From the sizing's open shares (a tree opens 0.58 of segments; carving brings it to 0.69), roughly 3.3 closed goal sides remain among about 18.5 closed segments, so k_r = 4 opens about 0.7 goal sides per maze and k_r = 2 about 0.35. An opened side makes the goal a through-corridor and can leave its ring as isolated posts. Island-safety still holds, but the family changes, and the design must say whether this is allowed. I recommend excluding the goals' sides from the pool and re-running the sizing (CPU only; the k_r = 2 and 4 rows will shift slightly).
- *Fragments.* In a perfect maze the wall graph with the boundary contracted is a tree (25 internal posts, 60 − 35 = 25 closed segments at c = 6), so every opening splits one component. Carving about 4.7 closed outward segments per goal yields the ring plus about 3.7 wall sub-trees hanging off the ring road's outer posts. These are islands too, some large. §5's two-way class (perimeter or island) therefore lumps goal rings with fragments. Make it three-way: perimeter, goal ring (A, B), other island.
- *Does it keep enough maze?* Yes, at k_r = 0: the roughly 6.5 cycles are all at the two roundabouts and their fragments, and the tree is intact elsewhere. That is what the design wants: the perimeter circuit still traces the maze between the goals and never enters a ring. The smallest-k_r rule protects this.
- *The sizing script measures what §2 says:* interior pairs at Chebyshev ≥ 2, the carving, the k_r openings, island-safety, no shared component, distance on the final graph, a spawn cell. Every table number matches `e3d-sizing.json`, and 362 of 368 is right. Two wording points: "(of 73)" should read "73 of the 78 possible pairs seen", since the random rows and the carved lo = 3 rows both observed 78; and the placements are *drawn* uniformly but *accepted* non-uniformly (pairs at Manhattan distance 2 fail the lower bound more often). The effective count of about 64 says the distortion is mild, but §2 should say "accepted" rather than "drawn uniformly".
- *Keying:* in this family A and B are drawn before the walls are final, so they must be keyed by (run seed, maze id, redraw index), not by the episode as E3b-1's `place` keys them (`maze.py:180`). §2 implies it ("spawns drawn per episode") but should state it, and state whether A and B are swapped with probability one half as `place` does.

**4. The gate.**

- *Thresholds and the tree column:* the four numbers check out (5.766 at E3c RESULTS line 75, 16.98 at E3b-1 line 96, 34.5 at E3b-0 line 13, 5.118 at E3c line 73), and so do the ratios. But the round-trip wording is wrong by the codebase's own definition: a leg is a visit after the first (`maze_measures.py:5`) and a round trip is legs ≥ 2 (`scripts/e3c.py:203`), that is three visits. So 2.0 visits is one leg, not "one round trip", and 4.0 visits is three legs, not "two round trips". Fix the words, not the numbers.
- *Smallest k_r:* right, and the 0.5 floor is non-binding (0.73, 0.70, 0.65).
- *Confirmation benchmark:* right and conservative. State that the bootstrap recomputes the maximum inside each resample, rather than bootstrapping the argmax member.
- *The G1 readings:* the fourth ("any blind control with no island contact: impossible by construction") is false. The entrance gap is columns 4j+1 to 4j+3 and the head's 3 × 3 neighbourhood at the gap's centre column covers exactly those columns, all open; the block's centre cell is likewise all open. A wey entering along the centre line never records ring contact. Either compute goal-ring contact on the closed 5 × 5 block (then any visit implies contact, exactly) or drop "impossible". The first reading should also say "goal-ring contact", not "island contact", given the fragments.

It isolates the island effect from the size effect, makes the gate's tree column one block at one size, and gives the rule-7 tree leg real content. About 20% more plays; `project` will say whether it fits the 3 GPU-hours. Also say whether P-joint's 0.5× is per champion or the arm's mean.
