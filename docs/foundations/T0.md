## 1. Compute accounting

**The units, per category:**
- **World simulation:** counted in `World` itself.
  - Worlds built: `World.__init__`.
  - **World-ticks actually simulated:** `World.tick`. `run` stops early when every world is
    done, and only the ticks that ran count.
- **Neural updates:** counted in `Brain.step`, as **S × B × effective substeps** network updates.
  - B is the batch axis: worlds × weys in a rollout, 1 in the input-response and history probes.
  - Padding and dead slots that are computed still count.
  - The `substeps` override is respected.
  - Scripted brains count zero neural updates.
  - `scripts/bench_brain.py`'s `SparseBrain` bypasses `Brain.step`; it is a benchmark and is
    listed as uncounted.
- **Why these choke points:** many places build a `World` (13 call sites in 11 files at D071),
  including
  calibration, the exp02 behaviour probes, exp03 coverage and its stimulus bank, 02b's replays,
  coevolution, geometry, the viewers and the benchmarks. `_play` sees only some of them. Counting
  in `World` and `Brain` needs no list of exceptions.
  - **Tests check** the experiment paths: `evolve` (selection, holdout, snapshot), calibration,
    the input-response, history and behaviour probes, exp03 coverage and its stimulus bank,
    coevolution and geometry.
  - **Counted by construction but not tested** (amended in D071 and D072):
    - 02b's replays run inside its "probe" attempt;
    - the "final" category (set in exp02, `evolve_forage` and `experiment.py`). "tuning" is tested
      (D072);
    - the exp02 probes `valence_check`, `channel_dependence`, `gen0_scores` and
      `integrator_rescore`, and the calibration functions `raw_motor_magnitude` and
      `calibrate_in_world`: categorised by decorator, but untested;
    - the viewers (`watch`, `showcase`) and the benchmarks are not experiments and write no
      compute file. Their worlds and brains pass through the same hooks, **except
      `SparseBrain`**, which bypasses `Brain.step` (above).
  - **Not recorded at all:** the pilot and exploration scripts write no compute file, though they
    run real experimental compute: `experiments/02-screening/exploration/` and
    `experiments/03-generation0/timing.py`. **Future pilots run inside `accounting.attempt`**
    (Fable).

**Categories, and how they are counted:**
- The categories are selection, holdout, snapshot, final, calibration, probe, measure, tuning and
  other. "measure" was added in D068 for coverage, fitness measurement and reference scoring.
- Categories are set by a context manager. Nesting is resolved to the innermost category, so
  nothing is counted twice.
- A holdout evaluation triggered by a snapshot is one evaluation, counted once.
- **Test:** `evolve` puts nothing in "other".

**Time:**
- recorded as **synchronised wall time per category** (`torch.cuda.synchronize()` at category
  boundaries), and labelled that way. It is not GPU kernel time.
- It works on CPU, and the category is restored on exceptions. Both are tested.

**Where the counts go:**
- `RunResult.compute` holds a run's counts. The old `evaluations` field is kept unchanged
  (selection only), so existing records stay comparable, and is documented as such.
- **Experiment level:** each experiment script writes a `compute.json` next to its results,
  summing every process. That includes calibration attempts and retries, probes and tuning.
  exp03 never calls `evolve`, so this is where its accounting lives.
- **Test:** counts equal hand-computed counts for a small `evolve` run, a calibration and a probe.
- **Accounting is pure measurement** (Fable). Scores are bit-identical with the ledger on and off,
  and the ledger consumes no random numbers. Tested.

## 2. Genome and score pairing

- **Order and chunking:** a permuted population, and any `chunk_worlds`, give the same **full
  [strain, world] score arrays**, permuted identically. This holds in each execution mode's own
  terms (section 4).
- **Stochastic probes must pair too. Jitter currently does not** (Astra, reproduced).
  - The jitter generator is seeded once per run and draws noise by batch position. Reordering
    strains, or changing chunks, changes their sensory noise: one per-world score moved by 0.0053.
  - **Fix:** noise keyed by world id, tick and individual, as common random numbers across
    strains.
  - **The regression test first.** The fix changes jitter draws, so future jitter results are not
    bit-comparable with 02's.
  - The batching defect does not establish bias in 02's jitter means, and the intended
    expectation is unchanged. New draws give different realised estimates and intervals (D067).
  - **The draws changed twice:** at f6af625 (a flat index) and at 59217da (explicit
    coordinates). Jitter outputs are comparable only from 59217da on.
- **Serialised scores:**
  - **A latent mismatch.** `save_population` stores `fitness` = one best score *per generation*
    beside the final *population*. Nothing reads it yet, but it is not paired with the genomes.
    **Fix:** save the final per-strain scores as `fitness`, and the per-generation history under
    an explicit name. Test that they are index-aligned.
  - **The champion.** It is chosen by a second evaluation (`evolve.py:171-174`), on the same
    worlds as the last generation. Under default CUDA that can pick a different strain than the
    log's best. **Done (D066, D067):**
    - The champion is the final generation's logged best, reusing that evaluation.
    - `GenerationLog` records `best_sha256`, the full hash, not only the nickname.
    - A rigged-evaluator test fails if the old re-evaluation is restored.
    - Checked: the 135 committed champions that have a log file (plus 18 local-only, superseded ones
      in `runs/m5` and `runs/m4-pilot`, 153 in all; corrected in D084) match their log's final best
      *by nickname*. Old logs carry no full hashes, so this detects mismatches only at nickname
      resolution (about 12 bits). It is not proof that the old re-evaluation never picked another
      strain (Astra, Fable). The check is a test.
  - Snapshots and holdout values are tested for pairing to their genomes too.

## 3. Complete inheritance

- **`Genome.cat` and `Genome.assign`,** which iterate over the tensor fields. Every hand-built
  `Genome(...)` outside `brain.py` moves to them: `evolve.py`, `coevolve.py` (two of which drop
  `dale_sign`), `exp02/probes.py` and `deletion.py`.
- **A scan test** fails if new code builds `Genome(...)` outside `brain.py`.
- **The inheritance test enumerates the fields itself,** with distinguishable values in every field
  (initial gaps are identical across strains, so they are overwritten), and Dale on and off.
- **Operations covered:** `select`, `clone`, `mutate` (unmutated fields unchanged), `breed` (elites
  and children), `_breed_islands`, `_migrate`, `cat`, `assign`, save and load. The test also
  checks that outputs do not alias their parents.
- **Integrity on load:** `genome_sha256`, which is written already, is checked when loading. The
  hash is extended to cover `dale_sign`.

## 4. Save, load and replay: three separate claims

1. **Round trip:** save then load preserves every tensor **exactly**, on any device.
2. **Re-simulation, same device and mode:**
   - exact on CPU;
   - exact on CUDA under the repo's defined exact mode, `replay_mode()` (`evo/bundle.py`,
     `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`).
3. **Historical replay** (qualified per Astra):
   - **Exact reproduction** of a historical score is expected only if the original run was
     deterministic, and only on its device, with its code version.
   - **Historical default-CUDA runs** are compared within a declared tolerance, and any divergence
     is reported.
   - **Comparisons use the original code version** (the run's commit), not the current code.
   - **Bundles do not record the deterministic-algorithms setting** (`bundle.py`). So no bundle
     is assumed to supply it: the mode is stated per comparison, from the run's records.
   - A replay on another device is not expected to be exact.

**Default (non-deterministic) CUDA: a tolerance declared per comparison, before measuring.**
- **Comparison:** the same genomes and worlds run twice on one GPU, and the same set under two
  chunkings.
- **Statistic:** the per-world score over the full episode.
  - T0 and T1 at their 02 settings: 200 ticks (`exp02/grid.py`).
  - Plus a 600-tick stress test, labelled as such (Astra).
- **Genomes:** 32 random genomes and 8 evolved champions (02's).
- **Worlds:** 16.
- **Repeats:** 3.
- **Hardware:** the one RTX 5080, with the pinned environment recorded.
- **Declared bound:** per-world |Δscore| ≤ **1e-4** for random genomes. For champions, the bound is
  also 1e-4, *provisionally*. The repo documents long-horizon divergence under default CUDA
  (`REPRODUCIBILITY.md`), and 02 measured a chaos floor of 0.0018 on an aggregate statistic.
- **Exceeding the bound is a finding, not a failure to hide.** It is recorded with its
  distribution, and the claim becomes "replay within X only under replay_mode". The bound is never
  widened silently.

## 5. Energy ledger and numerical checks

- **The energy-ledger bound, declared now:** relative error below **1e-5**, per world against its
  own starting energy, **at every tick**. That is the documented acceptance bound in
  `tests/test_world.py`.
  - `_play` currently returns only the final absolute residual, so it gains the per-tick maximum
    of the normalised error.
  - Residuals are checked for finiteness before aggregation, because `max(worst, NaN)` can keep an
    earlier finite value.
- **Numbers:** no NaN or Inf in scores, energies or brain states over a full episode. This is
  checked for random genomes at the mutation bounds, the extreme genomes in `tests/test_brain.py`,
  and evolved champions.
- **The suite:** N2, SH and RD, 8 worlds each, at T0 and T1 settings.

## 6. Island follow-ups (should-fix from the review of D064)

- **Validate supported island settings** at the top of `evolve`. Errors:
  - empty islands;
  - `migrate_every` = 0;
  - `migrants` greater than half the smallest island. Between half an island and its full size,
    an island would overwrite its own top strains with immigrants (Fable).

  An uneven population no longer gives a shape mismatch (Astra reproduced one with 5 strains, 2
  islands and 3 migrants).
- **Document the per-island elite and truncation rule,** which changes total selection pressure.
- **Stronger tests:**
  - a migrant survives breeding with every field intact;
  - all ring edges, and the untouched slots;
  - isolation over several generations (with zero sigma, every slot descends from its own
    island);
  - the log and snapshots still pair.
- **A single-island regression against a stored published log:** the first 2-3 generations of a
  published run, from its bundle config and seed, on its recorded device. This runs after 03r
  frees the GPU.
- **D064's wording is corrected** (Astra, Fable):
  - the stale Dale vector corrupts later mutation and clamping, not the migrant's immediate
    forward pass;
  - a stale migrant fitness leads to culling when truncation excludes the slot, not invariably;
  - the single-island evidence is Astra's parent-versus-current run, not the tautological
    `breed` comparison.

## Amendment, 2026-09-28 (D082): batch composition on CUDA

**The original contracts, kept as written above:**
- §2: any `chunk_worlds` gives identical scores, "in each execution mode's own terms";
- §4: exact re-simulation on CUDA under `replay_mode()`.

**Both failed on CUDA for one case.** A chunk that holds a **single strain** gives different
per-world scores from the same strain inside a larger chunk, in default mode and in `replay_mode`
alike. It happens in a few worlds, where chaotic dynamics amplify a rounding difference.

**The mechanism** is consistent with a different kernel path. A direct `torch.bmm` test shows a
batch of one strain differing from the same strain in a larger batch by 4.2e-5 at 64 rows and
6.5e-5 at 320 rows, with no difference at 16 rows. Batches of two or more agree exactly
(`docs/foundations/T0_gpu.json`, `bmm_cause`). At 200 ticks the effect reaches a few worlds; at 600
ticks, many.

**The amended contract, in the tested configurations:**
- **same batch composition:** exact on CUDA, in default and replay mode;
- **chunkings with two or more strains per chunk:** these agree exactly;
- **a single-strain chunk:** can differ, and is treated as its own composition;
- **CPU:** exact under any chunking.

The measured sizes are in `T0_gpu.json` (`composition`). This is recorded as the failure of the
original §2 and §4 contracts on CUDA, not as a widened bound. **No behaviour is changed in T0:**
padding single-strain evaluations would break the exact replays of 01b's single-strain champion
evaluations. Padding, or a guard, is a T1 decision with its own equivalence plan.

**Follow-up, 2026-09-28 (T1, D090).** Single-strain padding is in `Brain.step`, behind
`BrainConfig.pad_single_strain`. T1's class E test found:
- the composition effect is wider than stated above. At 1 and 16 rows per strain, the strain
  count matters even with two or more strains per chunk;
- reductions over fewer than 16 worlds differ.

So "chunkings with two or more strains per chunk agree exactly" holds as tested only at 8, 20,
64, 160, 320 and 1 280 rows per strain. See `docs/foundations/T1.md` §6.

**Correction, 2026-09-28 (D091).** The amended contract's line "CPU: exact under any chunking" is
too broad. On the CPU, a brain batch of one differed from its batch member at 5 rows. The CPU
rollouts tested matched exactly (`T1_diagnostics.json`, `T1_equivalence_v2_compare-cpu.json`). The
reductions statement above is refined in T1.md §7. The final food sums behind `eaten` differed on 8
worlds and on 1 world from the same sums in a 2 048-world batch, with the fields themselves
bit-identical. On random fields, sums over 2, 4, 8, 12 and 15 worlds differed and 1, 16, 20, 32 and
64 did not. See T1.md §7 for the
shapes tested.
