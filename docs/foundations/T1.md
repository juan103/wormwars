# T1: throughput, profile first (plan v2)

Roadmap v3, "Shared foundations":
- Profile to find the actual bottleneck, then tune chunk size, memory use and batching.
- Any implementation change gets an equivalence check against the previous engine, within a
  declared tolerance, recorded in DECISIONS.md.
- **Gate:** the next scheduled experiment fits its budget at the measured speed.
- A framework port is considered only if profiling points at the framework itself.

T0 left two inputs (D082, D083):
- **On CUDA a single-strain chunk is its own batch composition.** Its scores can differ from the
  same strain inside a larger chunk: up to 0.027 at 200 ticks, and 0.073 in the 600-tick stress
  test.
- **Padding or a guard is a T1 decision,** and the equivalence tolerance must be declared with
  that finding in hand.

**Review.** v1 (`98dc4f7`) was reviewed by Astra 6 and Fable 5.1 (`docs/reviews/*-T1/`). Both
answered "approve with changes", largely the same ones. v2 adopts all of them (D086):
- profile the world, host syncs, full runs, Task N's shape and single-strain shapes, with repeats;
- legacy-off loading for the padding switch;
- a wider class E test with an independent reference;
- no class N numbers yet;
- padding's implementation, accounting and a CPU-runnable test specified;
- a staged budget gate.

## 1. What the exploratory profile showed (2026-09-28, RTX 5080, torch 2.12 + CUDA 13.0)

These numbers come from scratch scripts on a clean tree at `baa9c56`, single timings without
repeats. T1.1 regenerates them from a committed script and replaces them. They are kept here only
because they shaped the plan.

**The workload:**
- 02's task T1 with N2: 302 neurons, 20 weys per world, 32 brain substeps per tick, 200 ticks.
- Evolution evaluates 32 strains × 8 worlds per generation: one chunk of 256 worlds, so B = 160
  rows (worlds × weys) per strain.
- **Single-strain evaluations happen during runs, not only at the end:**
  - 02's in-run checkpoints evaluate the current best on 16 worlds (B = 320,
    `scripts/exp02.py:341`, `evolve.py:149-156`);
  - final hold-outs evaluate one champion on 64 worlds (B = 1 280).

**Throughput** (strain-worlds per second, a whole 200-tick rollout, one timing each):

| Batch | Strain-worlds/s |
|---|---|
| 32 strains × 8 worlds (evolution, one chunk) | 139 |
| 32 strains × 64 worlds, chunk 256 | 137 |
| same, chunk 512 (the default) | 179 |
| same, chunk 1 024 | 190 |
| same, chunk 2 048 | 170 |
| 64 strains × 8 worlds, one chunk | 172 |
| 128 strains × 8 worlds, one chunk | 191 |
| 256 strains × 8 worlds, one chunk | 170 |

**Where the time goes:**
- **The brain step alone:**
  - 5.2 ms per tick at 32 strains × B 160, against about 9.3 ms for the whole tick;
  - about 20 ms of 27 ms at 16 strains × B 1 280.
  - These are timed outside the rollout, so they are estimates of its share, not attributions
    measured inside it (Astra).
- **`torch.bmm` is about 70% of kernel time:** two per substep, a dense 302 × 302 matrix per
  strain, cuBLAS's FP32 SIMT GEMM, at about 12-16 TFLOPS.
- **The rest of the tick is the world, and v1 misread it (Fable).** From evolution's batch to a
  4× larger one, the brain step costs 3.85× more and the rest only 1.7× more. So the brain is
  GPU-bound already, and the gain from larger batches comes from spreading the world's fixed
  per-tick cost.
  - v1's "the CPU is the limit" rested on CPU time exceeding GPU time in the profiler. That does
    not establish a cause: CPU time includes dispatch and waiting (Astra). T1.1 measures the world
    directly.
- **Memory:** peak 0.7 GiB at 256 strains × 8 worlds, in these configurations.

**The matrices are sparse:** chemical 4.1% non-zero (3 709 synapses), gap 2.4% (1 091 junctions),
maximum in-degree 65 and 50.

## 2. Levers measured, and the decisions

Bit-identical is the bar for anything adopted without a separate plan (§3).

| Lever | Speed | Result versus now | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Several runs' populations in one batch** (R runs of 32 strains × 8 worlds) | ×1.23 at R = 2, ×1.36 at R = 4, ×1.22 at R = 8 | Not yet known. T0 tested at most 32 strains per chunk; T1.2's sweep adds 64, 128 and 256 | Build only if a budget needs it (T1.3) |
| **Chunk size 1 024** instead of 512 | ×1.06 for large evaluations; none for evolution | Bit-identical in the tested cases, if no chunk holds a single strain | Keep 512; 1 024 stays an option per experiment |
| **CUDA graph of the substep loop** | ×1.04-1.11 on the brain step, less on a rollout | Bit-identical | Deferred: small gain for the complexity |
| **Several processes on the GPU at once** | none (1-4 processes: 139, 144, 145, 144 in total) | Bit-identical | Rejected for this workload. No benefit measured; the cause is not established |
| **One fused GEMM**, `[tanh v, v] @ [M; G]` | none | Differs at about 1e-6 per tick | Rejected |
| **Padding N to 304, 320 or 384; transposed storage** | none, or slower | Bit-identical | Rejected |
| **TF32 tensor cores** | ×1.2-1.6 on the brain step | Differs at about 1e-3 per tick | Rejected: a precision change |
| **cuSPARSE CSR** (block-diagonal over strains) | ×1.4-1.8 on the brain step | Differs at about 1e-6, and differed **between repeats of the same call** | Rejected: not repeatable |

**The cuSPARSE finding, scoped (Astra).**
- **What it covers:** this implementation (`torch.sparse.mm`, block-diagonal CSR), the connectome's
  matrices (up to 65 non-zeros per row), and this environment.
- **Repeats disagreed:** repeated calls with the same inputs gave different results. A synthetic
  matrix with about 12 per row was repeatable.
- **What it does not show:** that every sparse implementation is non-deterministic.
- **`replay_mode()` did not flag it,** but `replay_mode` defaults to warning only. T1.1 reruns the
  check with `replay_mode(warn=False)`.
- **No production code uses sparse matmul.** `scripts/bench_brain.py`'s `SparseBrain` is a
  benchmark.

**Conclusions:**
- **These measurements do not justify a framework port.** Dispatch and launch overhead are where
  execution strategy could matter, and T1.1 measures them (Astra).
- **The largest candidate measured** is batching several runs, at about ×1.35 (T1.1's profile);
  whether it is exact is not yet tested (T1.3).
- **No lever found closes 03a's gap.** 03a needs about ×2.5 over measured throughput
  (ROADMAP.md, "About 5 times"); the largest candidate is about ×1.35 (Fable). 03a stays
  unscheduled, as the owner decided.

## 3. The equivalence contract

**Class E: changes claimed to be exact.** Chunk size, batching runs, and single-strain padding
(T1.2), each against the evaluation it claims to reproduce.
- **Tolerance zero.** Compared per world:
  - score, energy, alive count, eaten, pellets eaten, starting food;
  - for paths without a world (the probes), the brain states.
- **Recorded as bit-equality:** the number of unequal values, and the maximum difference. Not "over
  1e-4": T0_gpu.json holds a 2.4e-7 difference counted as zero worlds (Fable).
- **Old against new on each device separately,** in the pinned environment. Never CPU against CUDA
  (Astra).
- **An independent reference.** Reference outputs are produced by the **pre-change commit**, in a
  worktree, and saved. Two runs of the changed engine only show internal consistency (Astra).
- **Shapes** (exactness is shape-dependent: no difference at 16 rows, one at 64):
  - rows per strain B = 16, 20, 160, 320 and 1 280. 20 is one wey × 20 worlds, Task N's shape;
  - strains per chunk 1, 2, 3, 4, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256.
- **Scope, stated:** it covers strains per chunk. **Worlds per strain** is a separate composition
  and is not claimed; `tests/test_brain.py` asserts only a tolerance for splitting the wey axis.
- **Modes:**
  - default mode and `replay_mode(warn=False)`;
  - the replay leg runs in a **fresh process**, because `replay_mode` sets
    `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG` after cuBLAS may already be initialised (Fable).
- **A mismatch in default mode** is checked first against the unchanged engine repeating on that
  case. If the unchanged engine does not repeat, the case is reported as non-repeatable, not as the
  change's failure. Otherwise the change fails.
- **A mismatch fails the change.** It is not reclassified as class N afterwards.

**Class N: changes that alter floating-point results** (fused GEMMs, TF32, a different summation
order).
- **None is adopted in T1.** Because the dynamics are chaotic, per-world agreement cannot be
  expected.
- **Any later proposal needs its own plan, declared before it runs:**
  - the scientific endpoint and its unit of analysis;
  - margins justified against that endpoint and the measured chaos floor;
  - an uncertainty analysis that handles shared worlds and repeated measurements;
  - the engine recorded in every bundle, and used only for new experiments.
- **v1's numbers are withdrawn** (both reviewers):
  - the per-step 1e-5 bound sat between the measured 1e-6 and 1e-3, so it amounted to excluding
    TF32 without saying so;
  - a ±0.01 energy margin does not transfer to Task N's arrival counts;
  - "the champion's rank unchanged" cannot be evaluated once runs diverge.

## 4. The work

### T1.1 A committed profile

`scripts/t1_profile.py` writes `docs/foundations/T1_profile.json`:
- **provenance at the start;** it refuses a dirty tree, as the T0 GPU checks do;
- **run under `accounting.attempt`.**

**Timing rules:**
- synchronised wall-clock time is the authority;
- warm-up at the same shape;
- 5 repeats, reporting the median and range;
- the actual ticks executed;
- peak allocated and reserved memory;
- TF32 and deterministic settings recorded.

**It measures:**
- **Throughput:** §1's table; the brain step's levers (§2), including padding to 384 and
  transposed storage; the cuSPARSE check under `replay_mode(warn=False)`; concurrency.
- **The world:**
  - a whole tick with `ScriptedBrain` (no neural work), against the same tick with the brain;
  - the world's parts: sensing, motor, movement, feeding and splatting, fields, and reaping. The
    two host syncs per tick are timed separately: `_reap`'s `bool(dying.any())`, `world.py:876`,
    and `done()`, `world.py:988-993`;
  - the pheromone splat and diffusion, run every tick although 02's T0 and T1 sense them at
    scale 0;
  - world construction, including the per-world map loop.
- **Single-strain shapes:** 1 strain × 16 worlds (checkpoints) and 1 × 64 (hold-outs), as a
  share of a whole run.
- **A whole short run:** a 02-style single-island run of 5 generations, including checkpoints, the
  final hold-out and logging. Its time per category comes from T0's accounting.
- **`replay_mode`'s cost** against default mode.
- **A Task N proxy.** Task N is not built yet (E1). The proxy is 02-T1 with one wey per world and
  300 ticks: same arena, pheromones off. Evolution-sized (32 strains × 8 worlds) and hold-out-sized.

§1 is then rewritten from the JSON. Decisions change if the numbers do.

### T1.2 Single-strain padding (the D082 decision)

**The decision.** `Brain.step` computes a batch of one strain as two identical copies and keeps
the first.
- **Why brain-level:** it covers every path that steps a brain, including the direct-step probes
  and `coevolve.play`, and does not duplicate the world. Padding in `rollout` would miss those. A
  guard would fire on every hold-out. Default-off leaves new experiments exposed (both reviewers).
- **What it claims:** a single-strain evaluation becomes exact against the same strain in a
  multi-strain batch. It deliberately **changes** earlier single-strain evaluations on CUDA. It is
  not behaviour-preserving for those.

**The switch: `BrainConfig.pad_single_strain`.**
- **Default on,** for new configs.
- **Off wherever a published result is read:**
  - **missing means off** when loading: `load_genome` (`genomes.py:201-204`), `Config.from_bundle`
    (`config.py:257-270`) and `brain_config_for`, following the `chem_direction` precedent;
  - **02's `grid.task_config` pins it off,** so 02's and 03's scripts reproduce their published
    evaluations. Probes that take the brain config from the script (`probes.py:148-151`) follow it.
- **New genome files and bundles store the value explicitly.**
- **All devices.** Padding is applied on CPU too, so one rule holds everywhere. CPU results are
  unchanged by it, because CPU chunking is exact.

**Implementation:**
- **When it applies:** the whole substep loop runs on the padded batch of two, only when S = 1 and
  the switch is on. Elementwise work is independent per strain.
- **Caching:** the doubled `W_drive` and `G` are built once and cached. `cut_gap` and anything else
  that edits `G` invalidates the cache.
- **Silencing masks and the `substeps` override** work unchanged.
- **Accounting:** `neural_updates` counts the computed work, the padded copy included, as T0.md
  says computed padding counts. A new field, `neural_padding`, counts the padded share. Unpadded
  work, comparable with earlier records, is the difference.

**Tests (class E):**
- **On CPU, in the suite, able to fail:**
  - with the switch on, the batch reaching `bmm` has 2 strains; with it off, 1 (a spy on
    `torch.bmm`);
  - results are identical on and off;
  - a genome file without the field loads with it off;
  - 02's config has it off;
  - the cache is invalidated by `cut_gap`;
  - the silencing mask and the `substeps` override still apply;
  - the accounting counts it.
- **On CUDA, in `scripts/t1_equivalence.py`** (§3's contract, against the pre-change commit's
  saved reference):
  - **switch on:** each single strain equals the same strain in its multi-strain batch;
  - **switch off:** each single strain equals the pre-change single-strain evaluation;
  - **multi-strain batches:** unchanged against the pre-change engine;
  - probes' brain states on the same three legs.
- **The sensitivity check (not a suite assertion; it depends on the environment):** T0's known
  failing case must still differ with the switch off. It is 02-T1 random genomes, a remainder of
  one strain, 200 ticks. If it no longer differs, the test cannot show that padding fixes
  anything, and that is reported.
- **Whether `bmm` is the only cause:**
  - if padding closes every single-strain case, `bmm` was the cause (the world's `conv2d` also
    sees a different number of worlds);
  - if it does not, that is diagnosed. Padding in `rollout` is not adopted automatically: it
    would need its own equivalence evidence (Astra).

**The published effect, measured (Fable).**
- Replay 02's final hold-outs and capability probes with the switch off, against the stored
  per-world scores. Both are single-strain (`exp02.py:356, 538`). This shows the legacy path is
  intact.
- Then report how much they change with it on.
- Published numbers are not changed. The difference is reported in DECISIONS.md and
  REPRODUCIBILITY.md.

**Documentation:** REPRODUCIBILITY.md and T0.md's amendment say what the switch does, and which
results were computed without it.

### T1.3 Batching several runs (built only if a budget needs it)

**The trigger:**
- **A measured, scientifically fixed experiment** (scope and cap declared first) does not fit its
  cap at the single-run speed, and batching's measured gain would close the shortfall.
- **The roadmap's rule comes first:** "shrink the experiment first".
- v1's second trigger ("over twice as long") is removed: it could not fire at a best gain of ×1.36
  (both reviewers).
- The decision and its numbers go in DECISIONS.md.

**If built, the scope is larger than v1 said** (both reviewers):
- **Runs keep their own training world ids.** `_play` crosses every strain with every world id,
  so the world batch becomes per-run.
- **The world takes a run seed per world,** used in map generation and in the keyed jitter noise.
- **Accounting per run.**
- **Early termination:** a combined batch can stop later than a run alone. The extra simulation is
  accounted, and runs that finish at different times are tested.
- **The test (class E):** every run's champions, snapshots, fitness, Dale signs and logs are
  bit-identical to the same run alone, with R = 1, 2 and 4 and unequal populations. Timing and
  provenance fields such as `elapsed_s` are excluded.
- Islands and coevolution are out of scope.

### T1.4 The gate, staged (both reviewers)

**T1's engineering closes when:**
- T1.1's measurements are committed;
- T1.2 passes its class E tests and the published-effect replay;
- the reviewers agree §2's decisions are recorded.

**The budget gate moves to E1, as a roadmap amendment:**
1. T1's close authorises a **bounded E1 pilot,** under a cap declared before it starts. Scripted
   navigators are not free: the world still costs time, and the pilot includes generation-0 neural
   genomes (`docs/E1/DESIGN.md:136-142`).
2. The pilot measures scripted and neural throughput on Task N itself.
3. **A committed budget calculation** for 04a and E2 lists tuning, training, checkpoints, final
   evaluations and diagnostics against an explicit cap, with headroom.
4. If it does not fit: shrink first, then T1.3, then reopen T1.

**Provisional arithmetic** (to be replaced by the pilot): a 02-style run is 40 generations × 32
strains × 8 worlds, about 10 240 strain-worlds, about 75 s at 139 per second. Checkpoints and
hold-outs come on top. T1.1's Task N proxy gives the Task N figure.

## 6. Results (2026-09-28; D090, for review)

### T1.1: the committed profile

`T1_profile.json`, a clean run at `101499c` with 5 repeats, agrees with §1's exploratory numbers
to within 3%. The figures that matter:

| Measure | 02-T1 (20 weys) | Task N proxy (1 wey, 300 ticks) |
|---|---|---|
| Evolution batch, 32 strains × 8 worlds (strain-worlds/s) | 140.8 | 118.8 |
| 4 runs batched together | 190.9 (×1.36) | 375.6 (×3.2) |
| 8 runs batched together | 170.9 | 493.4 (×4.2) |
| 32 × 64 worlds at chunk 2 048 | 171.5 | 862.1 |
| World's share of a tick (scripted controller against a brain) | 0.55 | 0.70 |
| Without the two host syncs per tick (diagnostic) | 9.27 → 8.95 ms | 7.15 → 7.22 ms |
| Short 5-generation run: checkpoints' share of the wall time | 3.0 of 13.6 s | 4.2 of 17.2 s |

Also measured:
- **`bmm`** is 69% of kernel time at the evolution batch.
- **`replay_mode`** costs ×1.16.
- **Processes at once:** 1 to 4 give 141-145 strain-worlds/s in total.
- **cuSPARSE** was not repeatable in either mode; `replay_mode(warn=False)` raised no error.

**Reading:**
- On Task N's shape, the world's fixed cost per tick, about 5 ms, dominates. Larger world batches
  help much more there (×4.2 with 8 runs batched), which is T1.3's case. The host syncs cost
  about 3% on 02-T1 and nothing on the proxy.
- Single-strain checkpoints take about a quarter of a short run. They are small batches, hence
  slow.

### T1.2: the class E test, as declared in §3

**The reference.** Saved from the pre-change commit `1598d56`. It repeated exactly within itself,
and default and `replay_mode` gave the same file. Before the change, 39 of 74 single-strain
outputs differed from the same strain in a batch (the sensitivity check).

**The legs** (`T1_equivalence_compare.json`, at `e3694a0`; identical in both modes):

| Leg | Outputs | Equal |
|---|---|---|
| Switch off equals the pre-change engine | 188 | **188** |
| Switch on: multi-strain outputs equal the pre-change engine | 114 | 112 |
| Switch on: each single strain equals the same strain in its batch | 74 | 66 |

- **The 2 multi-strain "failures" are a test error.** 256 strains in chunks of 3 leave a last
  chunk of one strain (256 = 85 × 3 + 1), so this case is a single-strain case. Rerun separately,
  with the switch on, it equals the full batch in score, energy and eaten. The script now
  classifies such remainders.
- **The 8 single-strain failures are real, with two causes, both diagnosed:**
  - **`bmm` at small row counts depends on the number of strains, not only on one versus many.**
    At 1 and 16 rows per strain, batches of 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 and 16 strains all differ from a
    batch of 32. At 8 rows none differs; at 20, 64 and 160 rows only a batch of one differs.
    So the padded single strain equals the two-strain composition, and at 16 rows that
    composition itself differs from 32. The pre-change reference shows it too: the proxy at
    16 worlds (16 rows) gives different results at 2 strains per chunk than at 32, in 153 of
    512 values. This caused the proxy's single-strain case (16 rows) and the brain-alone case
    at 1 row (4 variants).
  - **Reductions over fewer than 16 worlds differ from the same worlds' sums in a larger
    batch.** This is the world's own composition, which brain-level padding does not touch. It
    caused `eaten` at 8 worlds and at 1 world. Score and energy were equal there.

**By the rule declared in §3, the claim as made fails:** a mismatch is not reclassified. What
holds, as tested:
- **With the switch on,** a single strain's score and energy equal its in-batch values when it
  has at least 20 rows per strain (worlds × weys). That covers 02's checkpoints (320 rows) and
  hold-outs (1 280), and every single-strain case with 20 or more rows in the test.
- **Reductions over fewer than 16 worlds** can still differ in the last bits.
- **Below 20 rows,** results depend on the batch's strain count whatever padding does.

**The published effect** (`T1_equivalence_published.json`):
- With the switch off, all 388 of 02's stored per-world hold-out scores reproduce exactly.
- With it on, none changes. At 1 280 rows a single strain already matched its batch.

**The CPU.** On the CPU, a batch of one also differed from its batch member: 1.2e-7 after three
steps at 5 rows. With padding the two are equal (the suite's tests). So padding changes CPU
single-strain results too, contrary to §4's premise.

### What goes to review

1. **Adopt padding, on by default for new work,** with the narrowed claim above. It removes the
   effect D082 found, changes no published hold-out, and leaves the switch-off path identical.
2. **Amend the contract.** A composition is the number of strains per chunk, the rows per strain
   and the worlds per chunk. Equality across compositions is claimed only as tested: at least 20
   rows per strain, and reductions over at least 16 worlds.
3. **For E1 and 04a (one wey per world):** evolution at 8 worlds has 8 rows, where the raw
   `bmm` check found no dependence on the strain count. Checkpoints at 16 worlds (16 rows) are affected. Use at least 20
   worlds for them, or accept results specific to the composition. Padding rows up to 20 is
   possible, but would need its own test; it is not proposed here.
4. **Whether T1's engineering closes** on these results (T1.4).
